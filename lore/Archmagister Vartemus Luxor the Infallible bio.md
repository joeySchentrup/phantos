### **Archmagister Vartemus Luxor the Infallible (c. 1100–980 BC)**

**Summary:**\
Vartemus Luxor, widely known by the self-bestowed title *"the Infallible,"* was an archmagister, philosopher, and controversial critic of both arcane and religious tradition. Living over a millennium ago, during the decline of the High Drynell Period, Luxor’s writings mark a turning point in Hurley’s intellectual history—a shift from reverence of myth to rigorous, if often abrasive, skepticism.

### **Early Life and Education:**

Born in the coastal city of Ulven Thass during a period of relative peace and academic prosperity, Vartemus was the son of a minor bureaucrat and an herbalist. He showed early promise in magical geometry and metaphysical philosophy, and was admitted to the prestigious **Obsidian Collegium** in Heraklion at age 13. He became a full magister by 19, and by 25, had already authored five treatises, including the incendiary *On the Cultic Delusions of Flame-Based Sovereignty*.

### **The Collegium Years:**

Vartemus ascended to the rank of **Archmagister** by 37, a record at the time, and was known for reviving and reforming the Collegium’s curriculum to emphasize empirical spellcraft, logical dissection of folklore, and “unenchanted” historiography. He was infamous for public debates in which he routinely humiliated elders, saints, and bards, earning both accolades and enmity.

His lectures attracted students from across Hurley, though many left either spiritually demoralized or in awe of his relentless precision. He was excommunicated three times from various regional ecclesia, none of which he acknowledged.

### **Works and Legacy:**

Vartemus’s most enduring work is the *Treatise Against the Seven*, in which he challenges the authenticity of the Seven Imperial Artifacts and ridicules the notion of spiritually bonded relics. Other notable writings include:

- *An Argument Against Metaphysical Crowns*

- *The Rational Impossibility of Divine Favoritism*

- *Ten Thousand Inaccuracies in the Book of Saints, Vol. II*

- *Magic as Mistake: Why All True Power Comes from Charts, Not Chanting*

Though dismissed as cynical in his own time, later historians credit Luxor with preserving critical texts, documenting obscure magical errors, and igniting an academic tradition of artifact skepticism that influenced the short-lived Rationalist Reformation of 675 BC.

### **Death and Posthumous Reception:**

Vartemus Luxor disappeared at the age of 120 under mysterious circumstances. One unverified report claims he attempted to transmute his soul into a constellation “to escape the stupidity of this planet.” His tower in the Saltstone Cliffs was found empty, save for a single note reading, *“I told you so.”*

In modern times, he is both reviled and revered. While orthodox scholars decry his blasphemies, arcane empiricists hail him as the first true arcano-logician. Statues of him often appear defaced—either by disciples trying to “update his smirk” or by detractors attempting to melt his beard off.

**Known Alias:**\
*The Infallible* (self-declared), *The Enemy of Folklore*, *The Saltstone Skeptic*, *The Owl With No Nest*

**Quote Often (Mis)Attributed to Him:**

> “The gods may exist, but if they do, they must be embarrassed.”
