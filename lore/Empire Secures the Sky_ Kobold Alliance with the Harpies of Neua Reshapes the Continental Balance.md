# **Empire Secures the Sky: Kobold Alliance with the Harpies of Neua Reshapes the Continental Balance**

### ***By Marcellin Veyre, Foreign Affairs Correspondent***

**NEW KORLAND, NEUA** — In what may prove to be the most consequential diplomatic development on Neua since the Treaty of New Korland, the Kobold Empire has formally entered into an alliance with the Harpy Eyries of the continent’s mountainous interior. Imperial officials celebrated the agreement as a “mutual compact of security, transit, and sovereignty,” while Coalition leaders denounced it as a flagrant violation of the postwar settlement that divided Neua between the Empire and the Coalition.

Whatever language one prefers, the strategic meaning is unmistakable: the Empire has now extended its effective influence across the central spine of Neua, including territories nominally assigned to the Coalition under the Treaty of New Korland.

For generations, Imperial strategists have spoken approvingly of the older union between the Kobolds and the Lanyarui. That arrangement, forged in the Empire’s early age of expansion, created one of the most formidable geopolitical partnerships in Phanatosian history. The Kobolds commanded the land with their armies, fortresses, and geomantic engineering. The Lanyarui mastered the sea, opening trade routes, extending naval reach, and transforming the Empire from a powerful island kingdom into a true maritime force.

The Harpy alliance now completes what some Imperial commentators are already calling the **“Triune Dominion”**:

**The Kobolds hold the land.\
The Lanyarui hold the sea.\
The Harpies hold the sky.**

That phrase may sound like courtly rhetoric, but it captures a very real strategic revolution.

## **The Mountains No Longer Divide Neua**

The Harpies of Neua have long controlled the continent’s central heights in practice, even when foreign maps represented those regions as unclaimed or divided by treaty lines. Their cliff-cities, nesting towers, and seasonal flight corridors make them nearly impossible to dislodge by conventional military means. The mountains are not merely their home; they are their roadways, their watchtowers, and their fortresses.

Until now, both the Empire and the Coalition had treated the Harpies as a third power to be courted, placated, or ignored depending on circumstance. The new alliance ends that ambiguity.

According to Imperial statements, the Harpies will retain internal autonomy over their eyries and sacred peaks. In return, they will grant the Empire aerial passage, courier rights, mutual defense cooperation, and access to several high-altitude observation posts overlooking both halves of Neua. Though Imperial ministers insist that this does not constitute annexation, Coalition officials argue that such distinctions are meaningless when the outcome is the same: Imperial influence now sits astride the central mountains from which both eastern and western Neua may be watched.

The most controversial point is that several Harpy-aligned ranges extend into land that the Treaty of New Korland allotted to the Coalition. That treaty, negotiated after decades of mounting colonial tension, divided Neua into two recognized spheres: the eastern territories under the Kobold Empire’s New Korland administration, and the western territories assigned to the Coalition. The mountainous center was always the most difficult section to define, its passes poorly mapped and its inhabitants largely uninterested in foreign boundary commissions.

That ambiguity has now been exploited decisively by Korland.

## **Coalition Outrage — and Calls for War**

The Coalition’s response has been immediate and severe. Several delegates have accused the Empire of “treaty encirclement,” arguing that it has achieved by alliance what it had not dared attempt by invasion. Others have called openly for military mobilization.

Their anger is not merely about the mountains.

Earlier this year, a local landholder on the Coalition side of Neua donated a considerable tract of territory for the resettlement of Twili refugees displaced in the aftermath of the Great War. The Kobold Empire swiftly facilitated the relocation, framing the effort as a humanitarian act undertaken in cooperation with private generosity. Coalition leaders, however, denounced the settlement as an unacceptable Imperial intrusion into their allotted half of the continent, warning that Korland was using refugee relief as a means of planting influence beyond its treaty boundaries.

The crisis briefly threatened to become the first serious rupture in the Treaty of New Korland. That tension eased only after the Twili settlers issued a formal declaration of neutrality, stating that their new community would owe no military allegiance to either the Empire or the Coalition and would not serve as a political instrument of either power. The declaration blunted the Coalition’s accusations, though it did little to dispel its suspicion of Imperial motives.

The new alliance with the Harpies has reopened those anxieties with greater force.

By securing the Harpies, the Kobolds have not only gained influence across the mountainous interior; they have also placed a powerful allied people near the same region where the Twili refugees now reside. Coalition officials argue that, whatever the Twili community’s stated neutrality, the Empire has steadily constructed a political and strategic environment favorable to itself within Coalition-claimed territory. Aerial patrols from the mountains could observe caravan routes, river crossings, refugee corridors, and troop movements in ways ground forces never could. In the event of war, Harpy scouts would provide early warning, communication superiority, and the ability to harry armies long before they reached the central passes.

Coalition spokesmen have therefore declared that the alliance “destabilizes the peace of Neua.” Some have gone further, insisting that the Empire has rendered the Treaty of New Korland void in spirit, if not in letter.

Whether that fury will produce action is another matter.

## **A War the Coalition Cannot Easily Win**

For all the thunder coming from Coalition assemblies, one must ask the uncomfortable question: **do they truly intend to fight?**

The Kobold Empire is not the power it was before the Great War. It is greater.

Its assumption of control over Hurley after the war transformed it from a dominant regional empire into the central political force of the known world. It commands the old resources of Korland, the colonial wealth of New Korland, and the industrial, agricultural, and logistical capacities of an absorbed Hurley. Its allies are numerous, its military tradition unmatched, and its state apparatus disciplined to a degree the Coalition has never replicated.

The Coalition, by contrast, remains a confederated power: formidable in principle, divided in practice. It possesses capable fleets, prosperous ports, and no shortage of brave soldiers. But its members do not always share the same interests, and war against the Empire would demand unity, sacrifice, and confidence that the Harpies themselves have now chosen the opposite side.

It is therefore difficult to read the Coalition’s present calls for war as anything more than political theater—an effort to reassure western Neua, placate outraged ministers, and show resolve before a domestic audience increasingly anxious about Imperial expansion.

The Empire, for its part, appears to understand this. Its statements have been measured. Its officials have praised the Harpies’ wisdom, emphasized peace, and dismissed Coalition complaints as “misreadings of local sovereignty.” This is not the language of a state fearing war. It is the language of a state that believes its opponent will not dare begin one.

And under ordinary circumstances, that assessment would likely be correct.

## **The Question of the World Tree**

But these are not ordinary circumstances.

For months, rumors have circulated among explorers, Harpy pilgrims, and Coalition antiquarians that something ancient lies near the center of Neua—something referred to in fragmentary reports as a **World Tree**, or at least a tree of world-significance. Whether this is a literal divine arboreal structure, a magical convergence point, or merely the latest fantasy of treasure-seekers remains impossible to say. Yet the persistence of the reports has begun to attract serious attention.

The possibility matters because the known history of Phantos already gives world-trees, sacred trees, and root-bound sites immense religious and magical importance. In Hurlyan tradition, the Worldtree near the Lake of Mystery is associated with some of the deepest currents of sacred history and divine artifact lore. Even rumors of a comparable site on Neua would transform a territorial dispute into something much more dangerous.

If the Coalition becomes convinced that the Kobold–Harpy alliance places such a site under Imperial control, its calculation may change. Political embarrassment can be endured. Strategic disadvantage can sometimes be negotiated. But the loss of a world-historical religious or magical site—particularly one located in treaty-disputed territory—could become intolerable.

In such a case, the Coalition’s calls for war may cease to be empty display.

One can easily imagine the arguments now being made in closed chambers: that the Empire has moved too quickly; that the mountains must not be surrendered without inquiry; that a World Tree, if real, cannot be allowed to fall exclusively under Kobold protection; that the Twili refugees on Coalition soil would be dangerously exposed if the Empire gains sacred legitimacy, geographic dominance, and aerial supremacy all at once.

The alliance with the Harpies may therefore prove either the final stabilizing piece of Imperial control over Neua—or the spark that ends the Treaty of New Korland altogether.

## **A New Age of Imperial Geography**

For now, there is no war. There are envoys, denunciations, troop inspections, and speeches. The Empire has raised no invasion banner. The Coalition has crossed no frontier. The Harpies have not declared hostility toward any foreign nation, only friendship with one.

Yet history rarely turns only on declared wars. Sometimes it turns on the quiet moment when a map becomes obsolete.

That moment may have arrived.

The Kobolds once joined with the Lanyarui and became masters not only of islands, but of oceans. Now they have joined with the Harpies and placed their hand upon the skies of Neua. The Coalition may protest that nothing has changed. Every serious observer knows otherwise.

The center of Neua has shifted.

And with it, perhaps, the future of the continent.
