### **On the Seven Artifacts: A Treatise by Archmagister Vartemus Luxor the Infallible**

Ah, the Seven Artifacts! How quaint it is that the common rabble still whisper tales of these so-called “divine relics,” as if they were not merely the crude leftovers of a long-forgotten alchemical scavenger hunt. Permit me, Vartemus Luxor—Archmagister Emeritus of the Obsidian Collegium, Eternal Laureate of the 27th Circle, and wearer of three-and-a-half laurel crowns—to correct the historical record for those unfortunate enough to have been educated anywhere outside the capital’s true intelligentsia.

Let us begin, shall we?

### **Artifact I: The Lucky Pyramid of Petapo the Inflamed**

The first artifact, often misattributed to the rags-to-riches peasant-king *Metapo the Fortunate*, is nothing more than an oversized dice container crafted by the proto-gamblers of Old Drynell. Supposedly this “pyramid” whispered secrets of fate. Nonsense. What actually happened is Metapo tripped over it during a drunken stumble through the Weeping Hills, and mistook a sudden updraft of swamp gas as divine inspiration.

The Pyramid is clearly a glorified paperweight, and fortune has nothing to do with it. Luck, dear reader, is merely statistics in a wig.

### **Artifact II: The Circlet of “Insight” (or as I call it, the Tiara of Wishful Thinking)**

Saulous the “Wise” supposedly wore this silver circlet that allowed him to peer into the hearts of men. Ridiculous. It is common knowledge among educated circles that the Circlet is an antique eyewear adjuster from the Age of Ocular Ritualism. Peering into hearts? Please. I can barely peer into my intern’s soul, and he’s weeping next to me as I dictate this.

The Circlet’s only real function is inducing migraines via sympathetic cranial pressure. That’s why it was hidden in a library—nobody wanted to touch it again.

### **Artifact III: The Key of Jaleth, Who Believed in Peace (Ugh)**

The third item, the “Key of Concord,” allegedly turned enemies into friends and transformed prison riots into poetry readings. I dare say, I’ve seen more transformation in a moldy cheese wheel.

What no one realizes is that this "Key" opened precisely one door: the back chamber of the Vael-Turog wine cellar. Its magical effect was simply fermented generosity.

Jaleth’s pacifism was not divine, but the result of moderate tipsiness and chronic optimism—a dangerous combination that should have been medically discouraged.

### **Artifact IV: The Scales of Aeleth (Tyrant of Tedium)**

Balance is a lie. There, I said it. Aeleth and his “Scales of Equinox” were the bureaucratic equivalent of a wet blanket on a bonfire. The scales themselves, forged from lead and smugness, could detect guilt allegedly—but only in people with poor posture.

In truth, these scales were a novelty gift from the dwarves, intended as a joke. Tragically, Aeleth did not get the joke. He legislated for nine hours a day and outlawed musical scales in retaliation. Fascist.

### **Artifact V: The Eye of Susapeg (The Narcissist’s Monocle)**

Ah yes, the Fifth Artifact. The Eye of Apprehension. This one at least sounds sinister, I’ll give the legend that. But Susapeg didn’t “replace his own eye” in a bid for telepathy. He had a nasty bout of hay fever and scratched it while chasing butterflies.

The “Eye” is a polished beetle shell from the Insect Cults of East Uterect. It glows not from magic, but from phosphorescent larvae trapped within. The paranoia it induces is a well-documented allergic reaction.

As for seeing into minds? I too have that gift—by reading people's diaries. It’s hardly mystical.

### **Artifact VI: The Ring of Anzu, Queen of Poor Decisions**

Ah, Anzu. Who wouldn’t want to hang five pendulums from a finger like a demented jewelry enthusiast? The Ring of Seeking is less a mystical artifact and more a wearable abacus for indecisive aristocrats. Legend says it pointed to desire—but honestly, it swung wildly toward the nearest merchant stand.

What did Anzu do with this supposed font of desire-divination? She bankrupted the empire by buying libraries, tombs, and two mountains. Her advisors begged her to stop. She responded by forging a sixth pendulum to “find what cannot be found.” It pointed to a mirror. She stared into it for two weeks.

### **Artifact VII: The Rod of Ha-Des, a.k.a. “The Stick”**

Finally, we come to the most ludicrous of them all—the Rod of Dominion, the wand of the nameless terror, the stick of evil, blah blah blah. More like the misplaced curtain rod of a deranged interior decorator.

This artifact is said to have warped Ha-Des into a Twili husk-witch, but I contend it simply aggravated his latent melodrama. The rod’s only enchantment was a minor enchantment of command—unfortunately, aimed primarily at goats.

To be “possessed” by it was less a descent into madness and more a career pivot into avant-garde performance art.

### **Conclusion: Let Us Be Serious**

The Seven Artifacts, in summation, are nothing more than magical junk drawers, each turned legendary by a combination of bardic exaggeration, emotional instability, and tragic accessory misuse.

If one is truly interested in power, I recommend two things:

1.  Education.

2.  A strong immune system.

Artifacts are for people who don’t have either.

Yours in enlightened superiority,\
Archmagister Vartemus Luxor, Fifth-Eye of the Collegium, Master of Suspicion, Enemy of Folklore
