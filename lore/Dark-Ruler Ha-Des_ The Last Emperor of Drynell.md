# **Dark-Ruler Ha-Des**

## **The Last Emperor of Drynell**

There are names which history preserves because men wish to remember them. There are names which history preserves because men are afraid to forget them, and then there are names which history invents because the truth is too complicated. Ha-Des was such a name.

For thousands of years, children in Hurley were taught that the last Emperor of Drynell had been a monster. Mothers frightened disobedient children with stories of the Dark Ruler who could crawl into their dreams. Bards sang of a pale emperor who surrendered his flesh to shadow, enslaved the minds of his people, and ruled from a blackened throne until the whole of Drynell rose against him.

The old songs did not even remember his true name.That suited Alexander Drynell just fine, for if history had forgotten Alexander, then perhaps Alexander could forget himself, but before there was Ha-Des, before there was the Dark Ruler, before the burning palace and the shattered empire, there was only a boy whom one eccentric magical scientist insisted on calling Alexie, and Alexie had never wanted a crown.

## **I. The Children of Anzu**

Alexander Drynell was born into a family that had forgotten how to be a family. His grandmother was Anzu of the Ring, Empress of Drynell, Mistress of the Five Pendulums, heir to a dynasty that stretched backward through some of the greatest rulers the continent had ever known.

Alexander heard those names constantly as a child. Metapo, Saulous, Jaleth, Aeleth, Susapeg. They stared down from statues, tapestries, mosaics, tombs, and the endless genealogical charts which covered entire walls of the Imperial Palace, yet Alexander knew these people only as history.

The family around him was far less impressive. Anzu had become obsessed with the ancient artifacts of her ancestors. She believed that the greatness of Drynell had always risen and fallen according to the strength of those who wielded them. Therefore, she reasoned, the future of the dynasty depended upon cultivating new wielders. Every eligible child of the Imperial Household was given an unformed artifact. Some were cousins so distant that Alexander barely knew their names. Others were his siblings, uncles, aunts, nephews, nieces, and second cousins. All received the same ceremonial instruction.

The artifact was not a weapon, not yet. It was possibility: a blank vessel of mana, spirit, and will. As its bearer grew, so too would the artifact. Its eventual form would reflect something of the person who carried it. An artifact might become a sword, a key, a necklace, a shield, a mirror, a book, or something stranger still.

Alexander's became a rod; nobody was especially impressed. His cousin Caelius had formed a magnificent saber edged in blue flame before his twelfth birthday. His sister Merina's artifact had become a jeweled harp whose music could strengthen those who heard it. Another cousin could summon spectral beasts from a golden gauntlet. Alexander had a stick, at least, that was how the others described it. Anzu did not openly insult him. That would have required noticing him.

Every year, members of the Imperial Household participated in the Trials of Succession, a grand series of contests intended to determine who had most successfully cultivated the powers within their artifacts: combat, control, manifestation, mana capacity, spirit synchronization. Alexander hated almost all of it, especially the fighting.

The first time one of his spirits was struck during a training duel, Alexander stopped fighting entirely. His instructor screamed at him to continue. Alexander instead dropped his rod, ran across the arena, and knelt beside the injured spirit. Technically, the spirit could not die in the same manner as a mortal being, Alexander knew that, but it did not matter. She was hurt, so he helped her. His opponent won by default. Anzu was disappointed. Alexander considered the matter settled.

That was the first year. The second was much the same. Then the third. Then the fourth. His relatives grew stronger. Alexander grew stranger, for most members of the Imperial Household, the interior space of an artifact was little more than a chamber where spirits gathered between manifestations. Alexander's was becoming a world.

He built houses, not physically, precisely. Artifact spaces did not work according to the ordinary principles of matter, but intent had power there. So Alexander imagined rooms, the spirits filled them. He imagined streets, they wandered them. He imagined a garden because one spirit mentioned that she wondered what flowers smelled like, then there were flowers.

Alexander visited constantly. He learned the spirits' names. He listened to their arguments. He settled disputes between them. He attended imaginary birthdays whose dates the spirits had invented for themselves. When one wanted a library, Alexander created shelves. When another wanted a lake, he spent nearly three weeks figuring out how to shape enough internal mana to make water remain stable. His family trained armies, Alexander made friends. His tutors called it wasteful. His cousins called it childish. Anzu called it irrelevant. Someone else, however, called it fascinating.

## **II. The Magical Scientist**

Steve arrived at the palace wearing three different boots. Alexander noticed immediately. Most people would have noticed that there were only two feet involved, but the third boot was hanging from Steve's belt beside several glass vials, two spoons, a small blue crystal, a screwdriver, a feather, something that appeared to be a preserved frog, and an object Alexander was reasonably certain was ticking. He burst into Alexander's chambers without knocking.

"YOU!" Alexander looked behind himself. There was nobody there. "Yes, you!" Steve lunged forward, grabbed Alexander by the shoulders, stared directly into his eyes for several uncomfortable seconds, and then shouted: "You're Alexie!"

"My name is Alexander."

"Exactly! Alexie!"

"No."

"Wonderful. Glad we agree." Alexander stared at him. Steve released him and immediately began examining the rod. "So this is it?"

"This is what?"

"The artifact!" Steve moved his face so close to it that his nose nearly touched the surface. "Hmm. Stable resonance. Very stable. Suspiciously stable. How many spirits?"

Alexander hesitated. "Twenty-three."

Steve stopped. "Manifested? Currently? Total?"

Alexander thought. "Do you mean ones I can summon outside, or ones living within the artifact?"

Steve slowly turned toward him. "Living... within?"

"Yes."

"How many?"

"One hundred and eighty-six."

Steve dropped the rod. Alexander lunged to catch it, "Careful!"

Steve stared at him. Then he grinned. It was the most frightening expression Alexander had ever seen on another human being. "Oh, I am going to have so much fun with you."

Anzu had appointed Steve to strengthen her disappointing grandson. That was her first mistake because Steve quickly discovered that Alexander was not weak. He was, in several important respects, terrifyingly powerful; he simply had no interest in proving it. Steve spent three days studying the interior space of the Rod.

On the fourth day, he emerged laughing, "Do you have any idea what you've done?"

Alexander frowned, "I made a garden?"

"You made a civilization!" Steve paced excitedly across the room. "Artifacts normally operate by hierarchy. Wielder commands spirit. Spirit channels mana. Mana produces effect. Very straightforward. Very ~boring~." He pointed dramatically at Alexander. "You don't command them."

"No."

"You ask."

"Usually."

"And they listen."

"Usually."

"Because they like you."

"I should hope so."

Steve grabbed a handful of his own hair, "Do you understand how absurd that is?" Alexander did not. Steve explained: Mana passed more efficiently between spirits that acted voluntarily. An artifact wielder normally compensated for resistance through power and discipline. Alexander had almost no resistance because his spirits trusted him; more remarkably, they trusted one another. His inner realm had become so interconnected that mana could flow between hundreds of spiritual entities in complex patterns almost instantaneously. Alexander's relatives had constructed weapons. Alexander had accidentally constructed an ecosystem.

Steve knew of only one precedent, "Metapo," he whispered.

Alexander looked up, "The first emperor?"

"Probably. The records are terrible. Very dramatic. Lots of things about comets. But some of the oldest fragments suggest that Metapo didn't treat his spirits like servants either. More like companions." Steve tapped Alexander's rod. "Everyone forgot."

Alexander looked at it for a long time, "Perhaps they remembered the wrong part."

Steve's grin faded slightly, "Maybe."

Training changed after that. Steve never forced Alexander to hurt his spirits. Instead, he taught him coordination. One spirit moved while another strengthened it. Another altered the surrounding mana. Three more protected the first two. Alexander learned that he could manifest dozens simultaneously if he allowed them to distribute the burden amongst themselves. He learned that his rod was not merely a conduit through which he issued commands; it was a bridge.

And so he became stronger, quietly, dangerously. Anzu did not know. Steve recommended keeping it that way, "Your grandmother," Steve explained one evening, "has the personality of a starving Owl-Bear in a butcher shop."

"That is my grandmother."

"I know."

"You're employed by her."

"I know."

"She can execute you."

"I know."

Alexander sighed, "You enjoy this."

"Immensely." For perhaps the first time in his childhood, Alexander had an adult in his life whom he considered a friend.

## **III. The Empty Chairs**

The Trials continued. Alexander continued losing, sometimes deliberately, sometimes because winning required violence he did not wish to inflict, sometimes because he simply did not care. His relatives mocked him less as they grew older. They had other concerns. The competition was becoming severe. Anzu began personally receiving the winners. Those who demonstrated exceptional progress were invited into what she called the Higher Court. It was supposedly an honor, an opportunity to study directly beneath the Empress. Alexander's older brother went first, then a cousin, then his sister Merina. Alexander remembered hugging her the night before she left.

"You'll visit?"

"Of course."

"You promise?"

She rolled her eyes, "Alex, I'm moving three floors upward, not across the continent."

She never visited. When Alexander asked about her, officials said she was studying. When he asked to see her, he was told she was unavailable. When he asked Anzu, she simply said: "Merina has fulfilled her purpose." Alexander assumed she meant imperial service.

More chairs became empty. More bedrooms were sealed. More names disappeared from dinner invitations. Nobody spoke openly about it. The Imperial Household had never been particularly affectionate. Absence was easy to explain when relatives were governors, generals, ambassadors, mages, and officials scattered across the greatest empire Hurley had yet seen. Besides, Alexander had his spirits. He had Steve. He had his little world inside the Rod.

Years passed. Eventually, Alexander noticed something alarming. There were very few artifact wielders left.

"How many?" Steve asked.

Alexander counted, "Five."

The next year there were three, then two. Anzu and Alexander. Steve stopped joking.

## **IV. The Final Trial**

Anzu summoned her grandson into the Hall of Ascendance. Alexander entered carrying his Rod. His grandmother stood alone at the far end. She was old now, not frail, never frail, but old. The Ring sat upon her breast. Five pendulums hung from it, each moving independently despite the still air. Alexander bowed, "Grandmother."

"Alexander." She had rarely used his name. That frightened him more than anything else. "You have disappointed me for many years."

"I know."

"Your cousins surpassed you. Your siblings surpassed you. Children younger than you surpassed you."

"I know."

"And yet you remain."

Alexander's fingers tightened around the Rod, "I suppose I do."

Anzu smiled, "Then perhaps persistence is your virtue." Alexander did not answer. She raised her hand. The five pendulums began to swing. "Show me what you have become."

Alexander stared at her, "No."

The pendulums stopped. The entire hall seemed to stop with them. Anzu's voice became cold, "You refuse an Imperial command?"

"I don't want to fight you."

"Then fail." Alexander swallowed. Anzu smiled again, "That has always been your talent." Something about the words hurt more than he expected. Alexander raised the Rod.

The duel began. He began losing immediately. Anzu's first spirit tore through his defense. The second shattered one of his manifestations. The third caught Alexander across the chest and sent him tumbling over the polished floor. His spirits cried out through the bond. Alexander called them back.

Anzu laughed, "You protect them?"

"They are hurt."

"They are tools."

"They are alive."

"They are mana wearing faces."

Alexander rose. Another attack struck him. He blocked badly. Pain flashed up his arm, then Anzu summoned another spirit. Alexander froze. He knew her. A woman in silver armor carrying a curved blade. For a moment he could not understand what he was seeing. Then the spirit turned. Her face was distorted by Anzu's magic, pale and translucent, but Alexander knew that face; he had seen her manifested a hundred times, "Merina?" Anzu attacked. Alexander barely moved. The spirit struck his shoulder. He fell, "Merina!" The spirit recoiled. Recognition flickered across her face, then the Ring pulled her backward.

Alexander stared at his grandmother, "What did you do?"

Anzu said nothing.

Another spirit appeared. His brother's. Then his cousin's. Then another. And another. People Alexander had grown up beside. Spirits he had watched develop alongside their wielders. Companions who should have vanished when their artifacts were sealed. They were all here, bound to Anzu. Alexander's stomach turned, "No."

The Ring opened. For an instant Alexander perceived the interior space within it. He had expected something resembling his own artifact. A palace perhaps. A city. Rooms. Gardens. A court. Instead he saw darkness. A jail with thousands of fragments drifted through it. Broken spirits. Half-conscious. Bound together. Some were screaming. Others had forgotten how, and beneath them lay something worse: the residue of mortal mana. Familiar mana. His family.

Alexander understood. Artifacts were connected to their wielders, deeply. To tear away the spirits by force required tearing through that connection. Anzu had not merely stolen the spirits; She had consumed the mana of those bonded to them. She had drained them until nothing remained. The Higher Court had never existed. There had been no secluded training. No grand appointments. No secret service to the Empire.There had only been a graveyard.

"You killed them."

Anzu's expression remained perfectly calm, "They were weak."

"My brother."

"Weak."

"My cousins."

"Weak."

"Merina."

Anzu paused, "Promising."

Something broke inside Alexander. For years his teachers had tried to make him angry enough to fight. They had failed. His cousins had insulted him. His instructors had humiliated him. Anzu had ignored him. None had succeeded. Now, for the first time in his life, Alexander wanted to hurt someone. His artifact felt it. One hundred and eighty-six spirits felt it. The entire inner world went silent. Alexander stood.

Anzu raised the Ring, "At last."

Alexander did not remember the next several minutes clearly. Neither did Steve, who observed from the sealed gallery above. He remembered light. Then darkness. Then hundreds of spirits pouring into the Hall of Ascendance simultaneously. He remembered Anzu's five pendulums swinging so violently that the Ring began cutting into her chest. He remembered Alexander screaming, not commands, but names. Every spirit he summoned came because Alexander asked. Every one struck willingly. Every one fought for the man who had treated them as family. Anzu commanded slaves. Alexander rallied friends.

It was not close. When it ended, Anzu lay against the steps of the Imperial throne. The Ring had fallen from her neck. Alexander stood twenty feet away, shaking, "Grandmother?" He had not meant to kill her. He had only wanted her to stop. Anzu looked down at the blood staining her robes.

Then she laughed. Not loudly. Not cruelly. Almost proudly"Well."

Alexander dropped the Rod. He rushed to her side, "Steve!" The doors burst open. Steve ran inside.

Anzu caught Alexander's wrist. Her grip remained astonishingly strong, "Don't.”

"You're hurt."

"I am dying."

"No."

"Alexander." For once, she sounded like his grandmother. Not his Empress. Not his judge. Not the woman who had murdered nearly every member of their family. Simply an old woman speaking to the last child of her house. She looked at him. Then toward the throne. "Congratulations..." Her breathing faltered. "...Emperor."

Alexander shook his head. "No."

Anzu smiled. "Heavy..." She coughed. "...is the Crown..." Her fingers loosened. "...that you now wear."

And Anzu of the Ring died. Alexander remained kneeling beside her for a very long time.

## **V. Emperor Alexander**

The coronation occurred thirteen days later. Alexander remembered almost none of it. There were trumpets. Incense. Banners. Oaths. A crown older than some nations was placed upon his head. Thousands cheered. Alexander looked into the crowd and searched for his family. Habit. There was nobody left. He was Emperor of Drynell. He was also the final surviving member of the Imperial Household. The Ring of Seeking disappeared before Anzu's funeral. Alexander personally carried it into the oldest vault beneath the palace.

Steve followed, "Are you sure?"

"Yes."

"Someone will look for it."

"Then they must not find it."

Alexander placed the Ring within a sealed reliquary. The pendulums still moved. He hated them. He ordered the chamber closed. Then he returned to the throne.

Alexander knew nothing about being emperor. This realization came quickly. He knew imperial history. He knew artifact theory. He knew ancient literature. He knew the names and personalities of two hundred spirits. He knew how Merina took her tea. He knew how his brother cheated at dice. He knew how his cousin Caelius laughed when frightened. He knew nothing about agricultural tariffs, or provincial debt, or grain reserves, or military appointments, or municipal codes, or irrigation, or diplomatic precedence, or the seven separate offices responsible for maintaining the Imperial road system.

On the first morning of his reign, Alexander received nine hundred and fourteen documents requiring Imperial review. He read eleven, then he vomited. His ministers assured him this was normal. They would help. At first, that seemed reasonable. The Imperial bureaucracy was ancient and competent. The Empire had functioned during sick emperors, child emperors, absent emperors, and foolish emperors. Surely it could survive an inexperienced one.

Alexander delegated. Then he delegated more. His ministers prepared summaries. Alexander approved them. Governors sent reports. Ministers condensed them. Alexander signed the recommendations. Petitions arrived. Secretaries categorized them. Alexander read only those deemed essential. The system worked, for a while.

Then the reports changed. Tax revenue increased, good. Production quotas improved, good. Banditry decreased, good. Provincial unrest was "contained," good. Alexander learned to love that word, contained. It meant he did not have to understand the problem. One governor requested permission to "relocate destabilizing populations." Alexander asked what that meant. His minister explained that troublesome groups would be transferred to settlements where they could be better administered. Alexander signed.

Months later, Steve showed him a letter describing entire villages forced from their homes. Alexander was horrified. "I didn't authorize this." Steve placed the decree in front of him. Alexander stared at his own signature. Another report mentioned emergency levies. Another described arrests of political agitators. Another authorized military seizure of grain during shortages. Another granted regional officials expanded powers to maintain order. Alexander began seeing what was happening, and he did nothing. Not because he approved, but because he was afraid.

Every time he summoned his ministers to confront them, they buried him beneath policy, population figures, trade projections, legal precedents, military necessities, competing provincial jurisdictions. The ministers always had an explanation. Alexander always felt stupid, so he withdrew. At first, only for hours, then days. Within the Rod, nobody expected him to understand tariffs. Nobody bowed. Nobody called him Majesty unless they were mocking him. The spirits welcomed him home. That was what it felt like: home. Outside waited Drynell. Inside waited family. Alexander chose inside. Again. And again. And again.

The Imperial Court learned that the Emperor could be avoided. Then it learned that the Emperor could be manipulated. Finally, it learned that the Emperor could be ignored. Provincial governors began acting as kings. Magistrates sold judgments. Tax collectors took twice what the treasury demanded. Soldiers became private enforcers. Officials seized land from political enemies and called it Imperial necessity. Those who protested were imprisoned in Alexander's name. Those who resisted were executed beneath Alexander's seal. He knew enough to understand that something terrible was happening. He did not know enough to know how to stop it. That became his excuse. Then his habit. Then his sin.

Years later, Alexander would name it without qualification: fear and sloth. Not the laziness of a man unwilling to move., but the sloth of a man unwilling to bear the burden of what he had been given. The officials committed the atrocities. Alexander allowed the officials to remain. Both things were true.

## **VI. Steve's Impossible Solution**

Steve watched his friend disappear, not physically, at least not yet. Alexander simply spent less and less time in the world. Sometimes Steve found him sitting against the Rod for hours, body motionless while his consciousness wandered its interior realm. Eventually Steve stopped pretending this was sustainable, "Alexie." No response, "Alexie." Nothing. Steve kicked him.

Alexander returned so quickly that he nearly fell from the chair, "What was that for?"

"Checking whether emperors still have shins." Alexander glared at him. Steve sat down. "You can't keep doing this."

"I know."

"You've said that for three years."

"I know."

"People are dying."

Alexander looked away, "I know."

Steve's anger faded. That was the problem. Alexander knew. He simply did not know what to do with the knowledge. "What do you want?" Steve asked.

Alexander looked toward the Rod, "To stay."

Steve followed his gaze. Inside. Of course. Steve should have dismissed it. Instead he said, "Maybe you can."

Alexander turned. That sentence changed history. Steve had spent decades studying the relationship between mortal consciousness, artifact spirits, and mana. Spirits could manifest physically. Mortals could enter artifact spaces mentally. So the obvious question, obvious to Steve, at least, was whether a mortal consciousness could permanently cross the boundary. There was one major obstacle. The mortal soul was not designed to exist independently of a living body. Twili, however, complicated that rule.

Their nature existed closer to the boundary between corporeal existence and spirit. Shadow and form. Presence and absence. Steve believed that if a mortal were first transformed into something approximating Twili physiology, then the soul could be loosened sufficiently from the body to cross into an artifact without dying. Probably

Alexander stared at him, "Probably?"

"Very probably."

"How probably?"

Steve hesitated, "Enough that I am willing to try it myself."

"No."

"Yes."

"You could die."

"That's the useful thing about experiments. Eventually one of the possibilities happens."

"Steve."

"Alexie."

"I hate when you call me that."

"I know."

Steve performed the ritual on himself first. It nearly killed him. For six days his body alternated between corporeal flesh and translucent shadow. For another three, he spoke backward. On the tenth day, he vanished. Alexander believed he was dead. Then Steve emerged from the Rod screaming, "IT WORKED!"

Alexander hit him. Then hugged him. Then hit him again. Steve had done it. He had crossed into the artifact as a spirit and returned. His body was altered. Twili-like. Shadow-touched, but alive. More importantly, the procedure could be repeated. Alexander hesitated, not because he feared death, death almost sounded restful. He hesitated because becoming a spirit meant abandoning what little remained of his reign.

"You are already abandoning it," Steve said. Alexander looked at him. Steve regretted the words immediately, but they were true. "You have two choices," Steve continued. "Stay here and actually rule, or leave."

"I don't know how to rule."

"Then learn."

"I've tried."

"No. You've hidden."

Alexander flinched. Steve hated himself for saying it. Alexander looked toward the palace windows. Beyond them stretched the capital of Drynell. Millions of people lived beneath the authority of his crown. Somewhere beyond those walls men were being arrested under laws he had signed without reading. Somewhere a family was losing its farm because an official had discovered another interpretation of a tax decree. Somewhere a governor was planning his own little kingdom.

Alexander closed his eyes. "I can't." Steve said nothing. "I can't do this." That was the answer, and perhaps the greatest failure of Alexander Drynell's life.

## **VII. The Making of Ha-Des**

The ritual was performed beneath the palace. Alexander told no ministers, no governors, no generals. He told himself secrecy was necessary. In truth, he was ashamed. Steve carved the sigils. Alexander lay within the ritual circle. The Rod rested beside him.

"Last chance," Steve said.

Alexander looked at his oldest friend, "Will it hurt?"

"Terribly."

"Good."

Steve frowned. "Don't say things like that."

Alexander looked away. "Sorry."

The ritual began. Twili magic entered his body first. Cold. Not the cold of winter. The cold of absence. Alexander felt his heartbeat slow, his skin paled, his senses stretched strangely into shadows around him. He screamed. Then came the separation. Every instinct in his body fought it. Flesh pulled one direction, soul another. His spirits reached for him from within the artifact. Alexander reached back. The Rod shook. The chamber went black, and Alexander Drynell died… for approximately four seconds, then something opened its eyes: pale blue skin, darkened eyes, elongated ears. A body altered by the magic of the Twili, a soul no longer seated entirely within flesh, Alexander lifted one shaking hand. He could see through it if he thought hard enough.

Steve began laughing and crying simultaneously, "Alexie," Alexander smiled weakly. "you look terrible."

"So do you."

They had succeeded. Unfortunately, the palace guard entered at precisely the wrong moment. What they saw was their Emperor standing within a forbidden circle, transformed into the likeness of the ancient enemies of Hurly, surrounded by shadow magic, beside a mad scientist who had performed the same transformation, well, let’s just say that rumor outran explanation. By sundown, the Emperor had sacrificed his humanity for immortality. By midnight, he had joined the Twili. By morning, he had sold Drynell to darkness.

The ministers moved immediately. Some genuinely believed Alexander had become a threat. Others saw opportunity. Emergency powers were declared. The Emperor was said to be compromised, then corrupted, then possessed. Imperial troops were ordered to secure the palace. Steve and Alexander fled. The Emperor of Drynell escaped his own capital through a servant's passage. He carried only the Rod.

Behind him, his ministers fought over what remained. The provinces erupted. Some revolted against corrupt governors. Some revolted against Alexander. Some revolted against both. Some governors declared that they alone remained loyal to the "true principles of Drynell," conveniently establishing autonomous states beneath their personal rule. Others claimed the Dark Emperor had bewitched the government.

Atrocities committed over decades were suddenly attributed directly to Alexander. Sometimes this was dishonest. Sometimes it was simply easier. The people knew that taxes had been taken in the Emperor's name. Their sons had been conscripted in the Emperor's name. Their neighbors had disappeared in the Emperor's name. Their protests had been crushed in the Emperor's name.

Whether Alexander had personally ordered those things mattered very little to someone standing beside a grave. The name Ha-Des began appearing in rebel proclamations: the Dark Ruler, the Shadow Emperor, the One Who Hid. Alexander heard it for the first time while sheltering with Steve in an abandoned monastery, he laughed, not because it was funny, but because the alternative was crying.

"Dark Ruler." Steve looked up. "They're calling me the Dark Ruler."

"They call me worse things." Steve quipped.

"I wasn't dark…" Alexander looked down at the Rod. "I just hid." And there was the terrible truth. He had hidden from his grandmother's cruelty. Hidden from the Trials. Hidden inside his artifact. Hidden from government. Hidden from his ministers. Hidden from rebellion. Now he had even hidden from flesh itself.

Alexander Drynell had spent his entire life retreating from things that frightened him. History merely gave the habit a title: Ha-Des.

## **VIII. The Shattering of Drynell**

The Empire did not fall in a day. Great empires rarely do. They rot. They fracture. They discover that borders exist only because everyone agrees to pretend they do. The Imperial governors became princes. The princes became kings. Military commanders claimed cities. Cities formed leagues. Provinces stopped sending taxes. Armies stopped obeying orders. The old Drynell roads filled with refugees. Some rebel groups searched for Alexander, believing that killing the Dark Ruler would end the suffering. Others searched for him because they wanted the legitimate Emperor restored. Alexander wanted neither, he refused to return.

"I would make it worse," he said. Steve did not disagree. That hurt most. The capital eventually fell. The Imperial Palace burned. Alexander watched the smoke from a distant ridge. He could have gone back. That thought tormented him for centuries afterward. Perhaps he could have rallied loyalists. Perhaps he could have exposed his officials. Perhaps he could have abdicated publicly. Perhaps he could have prevented one city from burning, one village, one execution, one death. Perhaps, but he did not.

He stood upon the ridge while Drynell died. The Rod hung at his side. Steve stood beside him. Neither spoke. At sunset, Alexander entered the artifact. This time he did not immediately return.

## **IX. The Legend**

The spirits were waiting. They knew what had happened. Of course they knew. Alexander had never truly been able to hide anything from them. The first spirit to approach him was one of the oldest. She had been with him since childhood. Alexander could not look at her, "I failed." She embraced him. That made it worse. "I failed all of them.” She said nothing. "My family died because of Anzu. My people suffered because of me." Alexander began to shake. "I knew." That was the part he could never escape. "I knew something was wrong." His voice broke. "I knew."

There were explanations. There were excuses. He was unprepared. He was traumatized. He was alone. He never wanted the throne. His ministers lied to him. His governors manipulated him. He had inherited a damaged Empire from a murderous grandmother. All of those things were true. None erased the other truth. He had worn the crown. The responsibility had been his. "I didn't want any of it."

The spirit held him tighter. "I didn't want to be Emperor." Others gathered around him. "I didn't want to be the last Drynell." His voice became smaller. "I didn't want to find out they were dead." More spirits came. "I didn't want to fight her." He wept openly now. "I didn't want to kill her."

The world within the Rod seemed to contract around him. "I didn't want..." He could not finish. So the spirits sat with him. Not subjects. Not soldiers. Not servants. Family. The only family Alexander Drynell had left.

Centuries passed. Then millennia. History did what history always does. It simplified. People remembered Anzu as greedy. That was close enough. They remembered that the last Emperor became Twili. They remembered that his government oppressed the people. They remembered that rebellion ended the Empire. All True.

Then the truths merged. Ha-Des became the architect of every cruelty committed during his reign. The frightened Emperor became a tyrant. The man who hid inside his artifact became a ruler who entered the minds of others. The transformation intended to let him become a spirit became a ritual by which he surrendered himself to darkness. The Rod through which he befriended spirits became the Rod of Dominion. The man least willing to command became remembered as the man who commanded absolutely. Perhaps that was unfair. Perhaps it was appropriate.

Alexander never decided. After all, legends are rarely concerned with the difference between doing evil and allowing evil to flourish beneath one's authority. The governors had ordered the killings, but Alexander had appointed the governors. The ministers had stolen from the people, but Alexander had retained the ministers. The soldiers had enforced unjust decrees, but they marched beneath Alexander's seal, so when later generations cursed Ha-Des, Alexander never corrected them.

He simply disappeared. Eventually, even he began to forget. He merged with the artifact, eventually losing parts of his ego, barely holding on to his consciousness, all the other spirits seemed to fade into the artifact, only he remained for millenia, He holed himself up in a cave and waited. Time behaved strangely for spirits. Years blurred. Names faded. Faces became impressions. The exact sequence of events dissolved. He remembered the important things. A family. A Rod. Steve. A duel. Anzu dying beneath the throne. A crown. Fear. Corruption. A ritual. Fire. Everything else became fog.

Perhaps that was mercy. Perhaps punishment. Alexander did not know. When at last he returned to the world, he chose another name: Myst. A small name. A quiet name. A name with no throne attached, yet the old name remained. Ha-Des, the Dark Ruler. And whenever Alexander heard it, he sometimes wondered which man they meant. The monster from the songs had never existed, but neither had the innocent man Alexander sometimes wished himself to have been. The truth lay somewhere more painful. He had been a frightened boy. He had been an extraordinary artifact wielder. He had been a grandson who discovered that his grandmother murdered his family. He had been a reluctant killer. He had been an unprepared emperor. He had been a negligent ruler. He had been a man who escaped responsibility rather than confront it. He had been the last Emperor of Drynell, and when the Empire needed its ruler most, Alexander Drynell had hidden. That was the darkness of Ha-Des. Not conquest. Not domination. Not cruelty. Absence. And of all the lies history told about him, that was the one truth he could never escape.
