In the Name of the Holy and Undivided Trinity—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Amen.

**I. Prologue: Concerning the Crisis and the Question**

Since the reconciliation of the peoples of Hurly and the devastation of war that stirred the consciences of all mortals, questions have arisen—weighty and worthy of holy contemplation—concerning the divine name of *Kalistos*, called in the ancient tongues the One-Before-the-First, Maker of the Heavens, Hidden Architect of Time. Many, both learned and simple, have asked whether Kalistos is indeed the same as the Triune God, whom we confess in the Nicene Creed, the Father Almighty, Creator of Heaven and Earth, and Jesus Christ, His Only-Begotten Son.

To answer this, We, assembled in sacred council with the College of High Bishops of Phanatos, convened in solemn session in the Basilica of the Divine Unity, do now declare in this Apostolic Constitution the true doctrine, inspired by Scripture, Tradition, and the discernment of the Spirit.

**II. On the Unity of God Across Worlds**

We profess, as it was from the beginning, that there is but *one God*, eternal, omnipotent, infinite in being and perfection, Creator of all things visible and invisible, who in the fullness of time became incarnate in the person of Jesus Christ for the salvation of the world.

We solemnly affirm: **Kalistos is the same God as the God of Abraham, of Isaac, of Jacob, and of Barron**. Though the name differs, the nature is one. Though His works appear diverse between Earth and Phanatos, His essence and His will remain ever unchanging.

**III. On the Divine Dispensation in Phanatos**

It has pleased God, in His infinite wisdom, to manifest His providence differently on this world, Phanatos, than He did on Earth. Whereas on Earth the Most High took an active and direct role in salvation history, speaking through prophets and becoming Incarnate in Jesus Christ, on Phanatos **He governed through intermediaries**, choosing to veil His majesty behind created agents.

Foremost among these are the *Six Primal Dragons,* *Fifteen Greater Dragons* and the *Twins*, who were shaped not by their own will, but were ordained by the will of Kalistos to serve as keepers of the world, guardians of time, life, and death.

Just as the angels were sent to Earth—Michael to battle, Gabriel to proclaim, and Raphael to heal—so too were these mighty spirits sent to Phanatos to guide, to guard, and to chastise.

**IV. On the Nature of the Dragons and the Twins**

We declare, by the authority given to the Apostolic See, that **the Greater Dragons and the Twins are not gods**. They are *created intelligences*, noble and glorious, but finite. They are to be reverenced for their obedience and majesty, but never adored. **Adoration (latria) is due to God alone**. To render such to a creature, however exalted, is idolatry.

However, we acknowledge the honor (dulia) owed to these beings as ministers of divine providence, much as we venerate the holy angels and the saints who have served the Lord with purity and awe.

**V. On Worship and the Sacred**

Let it be known throughout all the lands of Hurly and beyond: **No altar is to be erected to the Dragons, nor incense burned before them, save as signs of homage, not of worship**. Let hymns be sung in thanksgiving for their aid, and let statues stand in memory of their mighty acts, but let not the heart be lifted to them as to the source of all.

Only to Kalistos—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit—is sacrifice rightly offered.

**VI. On the Incarnation and the Hope of Redemption**

Though He has taken no flesh upon Phanatos as He did on Earth, yet the Son, the Eternal Word, is still Lord of this world. His Sacrifice is not bound by distance nor diminished by silence. **The Cross, planted in time, extends its shadow across the stars**, and the grace won by Christ on Earth flows to Phanatos through the hidden channels of divine mercy.

Therefore, let the Mass be celebrated, the sacraments administered, and the Name of Jesus proclaimed without fear.

**VII. Conclusion and Anathema**

Let no one say Kalistos is a different god. Let no one teach that the Dragons are to be worshipped. Let no one deny that Christ is Lord of Phanatos.

If anyone shall teach otherwise, let him be anathema.

Given under our Apostolic hand, with the seal of the Throne of Saint Peter, on this, the Feast of Saint Eustorgius of the Starry Isles, in the 2207th Year of Grace, in the City of Valemont.

**Papal-Vicar Victor I**\
Servant of the Servants of God
