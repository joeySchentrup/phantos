# The Greater Dragon Pantheon of Hurly

**Born of the Six Primal Dragons**, these 15 divine beings embody fusion elements—domains not of pure force, but of **balance, relationship, and transformation**. They are venerated across Hurly in various cults, shrines, and folk traditions.

###  **The Six Primal Dragons (The Divine Founders)**

- **Ouro’ras** – Dragon of **Light**, creator of humans and many sentient peoples.

- **Golestandt** – Dragon of **Darkness**, shaper of night creatures and Erosians.

- **Vlaurunga** – Dragon of **Fire**, mother of machines and metallic beings.

- **Yvander** – Dragon of **Ice**, mother of the seas and creatures of cold.

- **Quintara Lotus** – Dragon of **Arcane**, creator of magical beings and mysteries.

- **Rokesh** – Dragon of **Earth**, father of beasts, gems, and Kobolds.

# Hurlyan Calendar Overview

**Year Length:**\
≈ **364.3314 days**

### **Structure of the Year**

- **15 Months**, each with **4 weeks**

- **Each week has 6 days**

- **Total standard days:** 15 months × 4 weeks × 6 days = **360 days**

- **+ 4 Intercalary Days** (not part of any week or month)

- **+ 0.3314 Corrective Day** annually (accumulated into a leap-day every ~3 years)

### **Months of the Year**

Named after the 15 Greater Dragons of the Draconic Pantheon​Draconic Pantheon:

1.  **Jinshi** – Life

2.  **Agape** – Love

3.  **Caltheris** – Lightning

4.  **Zinareth** – Mischief

5.  **Tiraxis** – Paradox

6.  **Lurien** – Growth

7.  **Nekthar** – Destruction

8.  **Varkaleth** – Acid

9.  **Velcrin** – Secrets

10. **Dregmora** – Decay

11. **Volundra** – Storms

12. **Ignivar** – Alchemy

13. **Pyroxis** – Metal

14. **Cryomelle** – Time

15. **Glaedwyn** – Plants

### **Days of the Week**

Each week has **6 days**, named after the **Primal Dragons**​Story of the Two Childr…:

1.  **Ouroday** – Light (Ouro'ras)

2.  **Rokday** – Earth (Rokesh)

3.  **Yvanday** – Ice (Yvander)

4.  **Vlaurday** – Fire (Vlaurunga)

5.  **Lotusday** – Arcane (Quintara Lotus)

6.  **Golestday** – Dark (Golestandt)

### **Special Intercalary Days (Holy Days)**

Not counted as part of any week or month. These days are used for festivals, renewal, and divine worship:

1.  **Day of the Flame Crown** – Dedicated to **Kalistos**, the Creator God. Marks the **new year** with reflection and gratitude.

2.  **Day of the First Light** – Celebrates **Quint**, guardian of Day and joy. A festival of sunrise, laughter, and hope.

3.  **Day of the Deep Veil** – Honors **Erosia**, the shadowed Twin. A solemn twilight vigil and dream rite.

4.  **Day of the Breath Eternal** – Second holy day of **Kalistos**. Celebrated in midsummer as the "Sigh of Creation", focusing on meditation and cosmic harmony.

### **Corrective Days (Leap System)**

- Each year contains 364.3314 days.

- A **corrective "Kalistian Day"** is added roughly **every 3 years**, also dedicated to **Kalistos**.

- These are not named, but simply referred to as **“Day of Realignment.”**

# The Story of the Two Children

Our Story begins at the dawn of time for this little planet, when all that roamed here where the six primordial dragons-Ouro'ras, Rokesh, Yvander, Vlaurunga, Quintara Lotus, and Golestandt-, who where given this world by Kallisto, the creator. The dragons soon grew lonely however, and thus used their powerful magic to bring life into this world. Rokesh used his Earth magic to bring to life all the beasts and wild creatures of the land. Yvander used her Ice magic to create all the creature of the sea. Next, Quintara Lotus used her Arcane magic to create all the creatures born of magic. Then Vlaurunga used her Fire magic to create the creatures of metal and machine. Finally Ouro'ras with his Light magic created the sentient people of the world and Golestandt with his Dark magic created all the creatures of the night. After they had finished this, the primordial six were tired and decided that they should use their powers to create two final beings, one to rule the day and one to rule the night. These two were named Quint, day, and Erosia, night.

Many years past as this continued to unfold. The dragons were pleased with their work and remained out of the affairs of their creations and begun to entertain each other. Quint and Erosia got along well, but Quint used his power excessively, almost as if he thought himself a fool. Meanwhile, the people all praised him, for they were of the day and not the night. As time grew on, Erosia grew jealous of her brother and plotted to overthrow him and take his power. She convinced the Twilight Elves, or the Twili, to join her in her mission.

Then, one day it happened. Erosia used her power to maintain the night and allowed the Twili to conquer the people of the day. Quint tried to stop this, but while Quint was fooling around with his powers, Erosia was honing hers. Erosia quickly beat Quint and he was forced to flee. Quint went to the dragons for help and they gave him this: “Amongst our creation shall be born those who are destined to be our champions and shall help you in your struggle, one who represents each of us.” And so Quint went to find the champions. Once he had, they went to face Erosia together. With their combined force, they managed to defeat Erosia and banish her and her Twili to the Twilight, where it would be near impossible to escape. Quint became th one ruler of both day and night and the champions were rewarded for their service.

But lo, a prophecy was foretold with Erosia’s defeat: “The one who was cast out from this world shall return when one of the light who follows the dark lights the bridge for darkness to return. Fear not though! For just as the champions battled Erosia before so too shall champions be born anew to face the foe who threatens all.”

-Miathra 5

# The Tale of the Thread and the Loom

Long before the drums of war and the twilight of exile, when the world was still young and newly green, the Six Primal Dragons beheld their work and saw it was good—but lonely.

So, from the breath of the morning and the hush of the night, they spun two beings not born, but *woven*: **Quint**, bright and laughing, and **Erosia**, soft and silent. They were not merely siblings; they were the *balance of the world*, each a mirror to the other. Where Quint danced, Erosia dreamed. Where Quint built, Erosia watched. And where Quint sang, Erosia whispered.

The dragons gave them the world to share—not to rule, but to *understand*. And so the Twins journeyed across creation to learn what it meant to be part of the great weave of life.

### **Chapter I: The Gift of the Sunleaf**

One day, as they walked through the Glimmering Glades, Quint saw a tree whose leaves shimmered gold. “This,” he laughed, “shall be the Sunleaf Tree! I name it mine!” And he coaxed it to bloom with jokes and song.

But when night came, the leaves drooped and dimmed. Erosia stepped forth and said nothing. She only knelt and breathed on the roots. The gold flickered back to life under the starlight.

“You wake it with laughter,” she said. “But I guard its dreams.”

From then on, the Sunleaf Tree opened by day and glowed by night.

### **Chapter II: The Festival of the Bridge**

Every year, the creatures of the day and night would gather at the Hour Between, when dusk meets dawn, to celebrate the Festival of the Bridge. The Twins were always the guests of honor.

One year, Quint arrived early and juggled fireflies, turning the sky into a dance of light. The people cheered and chanted his name. When Erosia arrived, the music faltered. Her steps were soft, her eyes hidden beneath a veil of silver mist.

None clapped. None bowed.

And yet, when the music faded and the stars began to dim, it was her voice—a low hum like wind through reeds—that rekindled their glow and hushed the crowd to awe.

But Quint, flushed with praise and not understanding silence, laughed too loud.

“She says nothing because she has nothing to say!” he teased, meaning no harm.

Erosia said nothing, but her eyes turned away.

### **Chapter III: The Mirror and the Fool**

One day, the Twins came upon an ancient mirror deep beneath the Lake of Mystery.

Quint looked in and saw a thousand versions of himself: some brave, some foolish, some crowned in gold, some weeping alone.

“Look!” he said. “Even my worst selves are charming.”

But when Erosia looked in, she saw nothing.

The mirror showed her no form, no shadow, no face.

“Do I not exist unless seen?” she whispered.

Quint, ever the fool, tried to joke: “Then let me be your reflection, sister!”

But she turned away. “I do not need to be seen. I need to be understood.”

### **Chapter IV: The Weave Begins to Fray**

Over time, the creatures of the world—especially the people of the day—came to worship Quint. They built sun-temples and sang morning prayers. They told stories where night meant fear, and silence meant weakness.

The Twili, Erosia’s beloved children, were shunned. Called ghosts, spies, monsters.

And Quint, busy playing at praise, did not notice his sister's sorrow.

But the Twilight Elves *did*.

They gathered around Erosia and whispered, “You gave them the cool of night, the gift of dreams. Yet they thank only the sun.”

Erosia wept, but her tears fell where no one saw.

### **Epilogue: The Loom Stills**

It is said that in those days, Erosia began weaving in secret. Not cloth, but *possibility*—a twilight realm where her children could live free from the scorn of daylight. It was not yet war. Not yet rebellion. It was… withdrawal.

And Quint, when he noticed her absence, finally turned and said:

“Sister?”

But the thread had already grown taut.

And one day, it would snap.

### **Moral of the Fable:**

*“A song without silence is noise. A world without shadow is blind. Even the brightest fool must learn to listen—or the mirror may crack.”*

# The Twilight Weave: A Twili Creation Myth

In the beginning, before shadow and starlight danced across the land, there was silence—and from that silence came Golestandt, the Primal Dragon of Darkness. While his kin sculpted life from light and flame, sea and stone, Golestandt looked not outward, but inward. He listened to the hush between heartbeats, the stillness under mountains, the mystery of night.

From these, he wove his creations—creatures of dusk and silence, not meant for the burning sun, but for the cool serenity of shadow. Among them were the first of the Twili: beings not of one shape or place, but of transitions—between light and dark, between seen and unseen, between the breath and the whisper.

These early Twili were born from pools of moonless water and cloaked mist. Golestandt did not teach them to speak as others did. Instead, he gave them the language of silence, gesture, and dreaming. They walked softly, and wherever their feet touched, twilight followed.

When the Six Primal Dragons gathered and poured their final magics into the world, Golestandt offered something special. While others made dragons of fusion and flame, he instead whispered into the void and called forth two children—one of blazing joy, the other of quiet power as the culmination of all the powers of the 6 dragons.

The one born of day was Quint, the Radiant Fool. The one born of night was Erosia, the Hidden Mirror.

Quint laughed and danced through the sky, and mortals sang his name with trumpets and fire. Erosia walked unseen, guiding dreams, wrapping the world in starlight, and cradling secrets. The Twili beheld her not as a god, but as a sister. For she, too, belonged to the space in-between.

Erosia loved the Twili as Golestandt had. She saw in them a reflection of herself—misunderstood, unpraised, but powerful. She taught them to thrive in the darkness, not as exiles, but as chosen. She gifted them nocturnal vision, the ability to move through shadow, and most sacred of all, the Moonveil Rite—the power to become half-light, half-thought, disappearing where others would be seen.

But the people of the day feared the night. And they feared the Twili.

They called them spies, ghosts, monsters. They shut their gates at dusk and whispered lies about what slithered in the dark. And always, they worshiped Quint, whose light blinded them to what they could not understand.

Thus did Erosia grow sorrowful… and then angry. She whispered to the Twili a new dream—not of coexistence, but of reclamation. “Why must we be hidden?” she asked. “Why must night always yield to day?”

The Twili, loyal and proud, followed her into the first rebellion.

And so began the Erosian War, the first night that did not end. For a time, the world was cloaked in endless twilight. The Twili walked freely in cities that had long shunned them, their shadows swallowing the banners of day. But Quint, fool though he was, was not powerless. With the help of the Draconic Champions, he struck Erosia down, and she was banished to the Twilight Realm, taking her children with her.

Yet the Twili did not curse her for their exile. They did not forget her. In the endless dusk of that realm-between-worlds, they built their cities anew—woven from shadowstuff, starlight, and silence. They remembered Golestandt, their maker, and Erosia, their mother and queen. And they vowed to keep the night sacred, to hone their gifts, and to wait.

For prophecy says:

> *When one of the light who follows the dark lights the bridge for darkness to return, Erosia shall rise again. And with her, the Twili shall walk freely beneath both moon and sun, as was always their birthright.*

Until then, they train in shadow. They watch. They wait.

And when they move, even the stars hold their breath.

# Pierres d’Amour

A Legend from the Scriptures of the Kobolds, translated into Common

## First Story:

Long ago, just after the Great Hatching, The Divine Dragons, after creating the Twins, decided to create 15 lesser dragons that were combinations of their respective elements to rule over the combination magics. The first fourteen dragons were created, with the daughter of Ouro'ras and Golestandt, Jinshi: the Greater Dragon of Life, was appointed as the leader of these Greater Dragons. However, the last dragon took longer to be created, for Quintara Lotus truly loved Rokesh and wished not to create just any dragon to be their child, but rather wished to create a true child between them. So, to do this, Lotus created a gift to give to Rokesh, a creation using her power and the fruits of Rokesh’s power, and gave it as a gift to Rokesh’s beloved children, the Kobolds. These were the power stones, or in Kobold, the *pierres d’amour.*[^24]

When Lotus presented these to Rokesh, he loved them and, so also loved Lotus. The tow of them joined together and fell in love, being the only of the Divine Dragons to do so. Upon doing so, the fruit of their love was the last of the Greater Dragons, Agape: the Greater Dragon of Love. The two of them Loved their daughter, and their love shone through to the stones, which were empowered by their love.

Now these stones act as signs of love when given to other Kobolds and have an animalistic soul. These stones are treated like beloved pets by the Kobolds, and they frown on those who would use them for malevolent uses, for they should be treated with great respect.

## Second Story:[^25]

Long ago, in the age of dawn known as the Era of Awakening, when the world still trembled from the echoes of creation, the Divine Dragons emerged from the primordial forces to shape the realms. After the Great Hatching, the moment where the first conscious sparks lit the void, the Divine Dragons breathed life into existence, each embodying a primal element—Light, Dark, Fire, Ice, Arcane, and Earth.

Among their first creations were the Twins who acted as the keepers of the world's equilibrium. But even this was not enough, for the forces of the world were manifold, not separate, but interwoven. Thus, the Divine Dragons resolved to create fifteen Greater Dragons, each to embody and govern the sacred unions of elemental magic—fusion forces that danced between the lines of their original powers.

One by one, fourteen dragons were shaped from shared breath and magic, each the child of two Divine Dragons. Each dragon held domain over a specific combination magic: Lightning, Metal, Time, Plants, and so on. The leader among them was the wise and compassionate Jinshi, daughter of Ouro’ras, the Divine Dragon of Light, and Golestandt, the Divine Dragon of Darkness. Jinshi, bearing the radiant aspect of Life, was revered by all her siblings, for she did not only embody harmony—she nurtured it.

But the fifteenth seat remained empty.

This final dragon was to be born of Quintara Lotus, the Divine Dragon of the Arcane, and Rokesh, the Divine Dragon of Earth. Lotus, unlike her kin, did not wish to forge another being of elemental might alone. She had watched the others create their children with duty, power, and magic—but she longed for something more. Something real.

Lotus had long admired Rokesh from afar. His patience, his wisdom, his steady and enduring nature stirred something in her vast and secretive heart. And so, she resolved that their child would not be formed, but born—not merely a fusion of power, but the fruit of true love.

To express her affection, she created a gift—an offering not of grandeur, but of intimacy. Using her own Arcane-magic to shape the essence of love, and drawing upon the patient pulse of Rokesh's earthen strength, she crafted delicate, glowing stones—each one alive with a gentle warmth. These were the pierres d’amour—or “stones of love”, as the Kobolds, Rokesh’s cherished children, would come to call them.

She gave them not to Rokesh directly, but to his beloved Kobolds, knowing that he would see her heart in the act. The Kobolds were delighted with the stones, for each one shimmered with an inner soul, animalistic and affectionate, like tiny creatures of living gemstone. The Kobolds adored them, cherished them, and protected them as kin. And Rokesh saw. He understood. He felt. And for the first time, a Divine Dragon loved in return.

He joined with Lotus in harmony deeper than elemental bond. From their union—not from spell or summoning, but from true connection—was born the final Greater Dragon: Agape, the Dragon of Love.

Agape was unlike her siblings. Where the others ruled elements and forces, she ruled the unseen thread that tied all beings together. She could touch the hearts of mortals and gods alike. Flowers bloomed in her presence, even on stone. Beasts grew tame in her shadow. She could silence wars with a gaze, and inspire songs with a whisper.

Her presence did more than balance the others—it completed them.

And the pierres d’amour, now blessed by her very essence, were transformed. They became living relics, vessels of empathy and connection. Among the Kobolds, they grew into sacred companions—neither tools nor weapons, but beloved pets and spiritual kin. Each stone had its own will, its own subtle voice, and would only bond with those whose hearts were pure and intentions sincere.

The Kobolds, a humble and heartful people, came to believe that to give such a stone was the highest form of affection—be it friendship, familial love, or romantic devotion. To misuse a pierre d’amour, to treat it as a commodity or weapon, was to defile the love of the dragons themselves, a sacrilege punishable not by law, but by shame and exile.

Even now, in the modern age, when myths fade into story and dragons hide behind the veil of time, the Kobolds remember. They tell the tale of Agape, the Dragon of Love, and the stones that carry her soul.

And on quiet nights, when the wind hums through the caverns, they say you can hear her voice, soft and warm, calling out from the heart of the world.

# The Seven Emperors and the Lost Artifacts

A Short History

*A Folk Legend of the Drynell Empire*

> *“Seven crowns for seven hands,\
> Each to shape the heart of lands.\
> One rose lucky, one rose wise,\
> One with love that opened eyes.\
> One kept balance, one sought praise,\
> One grew greedy, one brought blaze.\
> Seven ruled, and seven fell—\
> Thus crumbled proud old Drynell.”*

### **Metapo the Fortunate Flame**

Long ago, when Hurley groaned under war’s cruel weight, a man named **Metapo** rose from the West. It’s said he was born beneath a comet’s light, wrapped in wind and prophecy. While others wielded blades, he found a thing shaped like a **pyramid**, smooth and golden, hidden beneath the roots of a shattered tree.

The old ones say the Pyramid whispered to him in dreams, led his feet where luck would follow, and gave him favor even in folly. Fortune bloomed where he walked, and his banner flew across the hills and valleys.

He almost brought unity to the land—but only almost. The final mountain held. His flame dimmed. Yet his name became a song.

### **Saulous the Thoughtful Son**

Where his father wore flame, **Saulous** wore frost. Quiet, listening, learning—he discovered an ancient **circlet** beneath a temple dome, said to be forged from the silver thoughts of stars.

With it, he saw not just what was—but what could be. He read hearts as scholars read scrolls. No war he waged, and none he needed. With wisdom like rivers and treaties like towers, he did what his father could not—he **united all of Hurley**.

They say two owls flew always above his head: one with eyes of night, one with eyes of noon.

### **Jaleth of the Kind Key**

Now comes **Jaleth**, the empress with hands of healing. She bore the **Key of Concord**, which could unlock the hearts of men—turning foes into friends, traitors into stewards, and prisoners into poets.

She found it nestled in the roots of the Worldtree, beside the Lake of Mystery, where spirits sing. They say when she spoke, even wolves listened; when she smiled, blades lowered.

Under her, Drynell knew peace deeper than still waters. And though her hands held no sword, her rule changed the world more than war ever had.

### **Aeleth the Keeper of Scales**

Then came **Aeleth**, who bore the **Scales of Equinox**. No joy in victory, no grief in loss—only balance. He ruled as if the sun and moon stood upon his shoulders. His law knew no favoritism, and his gaze was sharp but fair.

He could weigh the weight of guilt, and set right the wrongs none could see. He built courts of song and trial by truth. But the winds of balance are cold, and the hearts of men chafed under his even hand.

Still, his rule was stable. Fair. And finite.

### **Susapeg of the Watching Eye**

But now the tale turns dark.

**Susapeg**, heir of lines noble but thoughts troubled, feared whispers in the dark. They say he ripped out his own eye and replaced it with the **Eye of Apprehension**—a cursed gem that could see into minds.

He grew obsessed with others’ thoughts, their doubts, their secrets. He ruled not through justice, but suspicion. His halls were full of mirrors and masks. Every ally, a potential foe. Every smile, a lie.

The Eye blinked, and the Empire blinked with it—slowly, nervously, toward ruin.

### **Anzu of the Pendulums**

Then came **Anzu**, with fingers like claws and a hunger deeper than oceans. She forged a **Ring** with five pendulums, each swinging to a different desire—wealth, power, beauty, knowledge, and immortality. With it, she **found what she wanted**, always.

And what she wanted was **everything**.

She dug up the bones of her ancestors. Broke the seals on forbidden tomes. Stole even the Key of Concord and pried it apart to see how it worked. But the more she had, the more she lost—until all she could hear were the tick-tocks of those swinging desires.

Her court became a marketplace of ghosts. And her throne—hollow.

### **Ha-Des, the Dark Ruler**

And then... the one who should not have come.

His name is lost, forbidden, swallowed by time. We call him **Ha-Des**.\
He found a **Rod**, long hidden in the Twilight Wastes. With it, he ruled not by consent or fear—but by **domination**. He twisted minds. Broke wills. Turned saints into slaves.

They say he was once a man—but gave himself to the artifact, and it made him **Twili**, pale and shadow-bound. He declared himself god and cast long darkness over Hurley.

The people rose. The fires came. And **Drynell burned**.

### **The End and the Echo**

The empire shattered like glass underfoot. The **Seven Artifacts** were lost—hidden, broken, or sealed away by desperate hands. Some say their **spirits** still wander, whispering to new masters. Others say they’ll return when Hurley needs them—or when it is weakest.

> *“Seven crowns for seven hands,\
> Seven ghosts in seven lands.\
> One shall rise, and one shall fall—\
> When hearts forget, they lose it all.”*

# The Seven Emperors and the Lost Artifacts

A Quick Retelling

## **I. Metapo the Fortunate Flame**

*First Emperor of Drynell — Bearer of the Pyramid of Providence*

Long before the towers of Drynell cast their shadows across the continent, there was a boy born to the western coasts, in a village cradled between cliffs and crashing waves. His name was **Metapo**, and he was born beneath a moonless sky, the stars burning brighter than ever before. The midwives whispered that the night was a sign—that the heavens themselves had made room for a new light.

Metapo was not like other children. Where others tripped, he danced. Where crops failed, his field flourished. Where storms struck, he sailed unharmed. “The Flame that Luck Follows,” the villagers called him. But Metapo claimed none of it was his doing.

> “I do not chase fortune,” he said. “She walks with me, unseen.”

One fateful day, while hunting in the forest beyond the Weeping Hills, Metapo fell into a ravine. There, buried beneath twisted roots and blue moss, he found it: a golden **pyramid**, no taller than a man’s hand, pulsing faintly with inner light. When he touched it, the world went still. A windless hush fell upon the trees, and a voice—not heard, but felt—said only:

> *“You walk the thread of stars. Take what is offered.”*

From that moment, the **Pyramid of Providence** became his companion. He kept it near his heart, tied in a red cloth around his neck. Though no one ever saw it again, they all felt its presence. When Metapo spoke, the dice rolled his way. When he struck, the enemy’s sword slipped. When he parleyed, hearts softened.

With this blessing, Metapo gathered tribes beneath a banner of flame—red and gold, emblazoned with a single upward-pointing triangle. One by one, petty kings and lawless lands yielded. Even the kobold clans, fierce and proud, swore oaths at his feet. Never by fear. Always by admiration.

Bards say three spirits served him through the Pyramid, unseen but ever near:

- A **silver wolf**, who guided him through the wilds;

- A **skeleton in blue mail**, who stood between him and death;

- And a **silent storm**, who whispered truths when no counsel could be found.

But even the most fortunate flame can flicker. Metapo led his people to the foot of the **Verdant Mountains**, the last free stronghold of the East. He offered peace. They gave war. For the first time, the pyramid’s light dimmed. It is said the spirits warned him not to go—but he would not let pride halt destiny.

He fought. He lost. And though he lived, he never again carried the pyramid into battle.

In his final days, Metapo walked alone to the wilderness of the southern shore. There he left the pyramid in a circle of stones and whispered:

> “What was given in grace, I return in thanks. May one more worthy take it when the time is right.”

Then he vanished into the mists, and the Flame passed to his son.

## **II. Saulous the Thoughtful Son**

*Second Emperor of Drynell — Bearer of the Circlet of Insight*

When the Fortunate Flame dimmed, all Hurley wept. The great unifier was gone, and though the land bore his mark, his work was unfinished. The chieftains murmured, the nobles schemed, and the people waited—for fire, they believed, would return in the blood of his son.

That son was **Saulous**, the only son of Metapo. Yet where Metapo had been a tempest of will and charm, Saulous was like still water—deep, cold, and clear. From childhood, he was known for his silence. Not brooding, but listening. He would study the wind as others studied books, and when he spoke, people listened as if the world had paused to hear.

But even wise waters need a vessel. Saulous wandered after his father’s death, seeking what Metapo never found—understanding. He traveled east of the Verdant Mountains, into forbidden ruins of the First Era. In a shattered temple of moon-pale stone, beneath a roof open to the stars, he found it: a **circlet** of silver, etched with runes older than empire.

When he placed it upon his brow, the world shifted.

> “Not all knowledge is learned,” the Circlet whispered.\
> “Some is remembered. Some is yet to come.”

From that moment, Saulous bore **the Circlet of Insight**, a relic that deepened thought and widened vision. It did not speak aloud, but opened windows in his mind—into the past, the future, and the secret hearts of those around him. He foresaw alliances, plotted maps never drawn, and dreamed of towers rising in peace, not conquest.

While his father had nearly united Hurley by sword and luck, **Saulous did so with parchment and thought**. He drafted the **Articles of Unity**, weaving tribal customs into law and codifying justice. He established the first High Conclave, a council of many peoples. Under him, bridges were built where battlements had once risen.

His court was adorned with few luxuries, but many books, globes, and starscrolls. It is said that **two owls**—spirits bound to the Circlet—hovered ever near:

- One, called **Now**, saw clearly the present and whispered truths about what lay before the eyes;

- The other, named **Yet**, peered into coming days and warned of what might be.

These twin spirits aided Saulous not in domination, but discernment. He used them to govern wisely, never forcing the future but preparing for it.

He refused to take a queen, saying:

> “I am wedded to the dream my father began. Let that dream bear fruit before I sow another.”

Saulous reigned for fifty years. In that time, no war touched Hurley. It is said he foresaw even his own death, and welcomed it like an old friend, walking alone into the Tower of Aether to meet it.

He left the Circlet sealed in a star-map chamber beneath the library he founded, guarded by riddles none have since solved.

> *“The first burned brightly.\
> The second saw clearly.\
> The third will mend what was broken.”*

So the legend says. And thus, the people looked to the east for the one who would heal the wounds of rule—**Jaleth of the Gentle Key**.

## **III. Jaleth the Gentle**

*Third Empress of Drynell — Bearer of the Key of Concord*

When the Wise Son passed into the veil of starlight, the people feared the light of Drynell had passed with him. For who among men could match Saulous in wisdom, or Metapo in fire? The empire, now vast and prosperous, needed not a conqueror or a scholar—but a **healer**. The conquest was complete. The laws were set. But the hearts of the people—wounded by war, distrust, and division—still ached.

And so rose **Jaleth**, daughter of Saulous’ sister, born of the southern river-lands where lilies bloom beside temples and reeds whisper old songs. She was not raised for rule. She sang to the river and listened to the wind. She played with fisherfolk’s children and learned the lullabies of every tongue spoken beneath Drynell’s sun.

When the Conclave named her heir, many scoffed. “A girl of rivers?” “A heart too soft for crowns?” But Jaleth answered none of them. She simply said:

> “Let me find the Key.”

They say she dreamt of it long before she ever touched it—a **key of radiant light**, hidden deep within the earth, beneath the roots of Yggdrasil in the heart of the **Lake of Mystery**. With only three companions, she journeyed by boat into the mist, singing old songs to still the lake. There, at the tree’s base, she knelt and sang until dawn. When the sun rose, the bark of the tree split, and from within fell the **Key of Concord**.

It shimmered like starlight caught in water, and when she took it in hand, the wind itself sighed in relief.

> “This is the key to hearts,” it whispered.\
> “Use it not to command, but to awaken.”

The Key gave Jaleth no power to control—but to *open*. To make known what was hidden, to reveal goodness buried beneath bitterness, and to kindle virtue in even the most hardened soul. She walked unarmed into the dens of warlords and left with oaths of peace. She embraced rebels, spoke to bandits, wept with widows. And they changed.

The **spirits of the Key** were said to be **living flames**, soft and golden like doves made of fire. They circled her unseen, and when she sang, they descended upon the hearts of those nearby, lighting their conscience like kindling.

She established the **Great Reconciliation**, a yearly convocation where enemies broke bread and washed each other’s feet. She ordered the release of captives who had repented. She opened the **House of Returning**, where wayward citizens could begin anew.

But her gentleness was not weakness.

When generals urged her to crush a rebellious province by force, she replied:

> “If you break a branch to straighten it, what grows is twisted. But if you tend it patiently, it grows strong.”

Her reign became known as the **Golden Breath**—a time when art, music, and mercy flourished. Statues of her were carved not with a crown, but with an open hand.

She passed from the world in her sleep, under a flowering tree. The Key was never found afterward. Some say she left it beneath Yggdrasil again. Others say it returned to the lake, waiting for the next heart pure enough to wield it.

Thus ended the reign of the **Three of Light**: the Flame, the Eye, and the Key. But balance cannot hold without strain. And so came the fourth—**Aeleth of the Scales**—the one who stood between rising darkness and fading dawn.

## **IV. Aeleth of the Scales**

*Fourth Emperor of Drynell — Bearer of the Scales of Equinox*

After the passing of **Jaleth the Gentle**, the empire sighed—a long and grieving breath. Her reign had been soft rain on parched earth. But too much rain can rot the root, and whispers grew among Drynell’s cities: mercy without measure breeds lawlessness. Kindness without firmness cannot hold a crown.

Into this restless season stepped **Aeleth**, a cousin of Jaleth, trained not in riverside temples but in the fortress-courts of the north. Tall, solemn, and utterly still—Aeleth spoke little, but his silence was like a blade: sharp, reflective, and never wasted.

He claimed no right by blood, but when he arrived at the Hall of Decision, he placed an ancient relic upon the central stone: a pair of **scales**, forged from black iron and white gold, perfectly balanced, unmoved by wind or weight.

> “Let those who seek rule be weighed,” he said. “And let justice fall where it may.”

The **Scales of Equinox**, so he called them, had been found not by chance, but by calling. For many years he had walked the **Ashen Path**, a lonely road through the haunted plains of the forgotten kings. There, in the remnants of a ruined temple split by lightning, he found the scales resting atop a stone altar, untouched by time. When he lifted them, the clouds broke, and sun and moon appeared in the sky at once.

It is said that the Scales could weigh **not only gold, but guilt and grace**, truth and illusion. When a man lied before Aeleth, one side would dip. When justice was ignored, the scales would tremble. He judged no one without first placing the weights—sometimes literally—in his hands.

The spirits of the Scales appeared to no one but Aeleth. But legends describe them as **two figures**, always at his back:

- One cloaked in radiant white, whose footsteps sparked light wherever they touched;

- The other robed in velvet shadow, from whose eyes fell tears of midnight.

They whispered not words, but verdicts—felt in the marrow.

Under Aeleth, Drynell became an empire of **law and order**, where every village had a judge and every judgment was recorded. He restored discipline to the army, order to the markets, and dignity to the throne. His reign was **neither warm nor cold**—but constant, like the ticking of a great clock.

Yet, not all welcomed the balance.

The artists of Jaleth’s time now found their songs censored. The wild mystics who roamed freely under Saulous were told to register their visions. Even the humble traders began to dread the black wagons that carried the Imperial Scales to their towns.

> “The empire is upright,” Aeleth said, “but only because I hold the level.”

Some feared he had no heart. Others feared he had one—but buried too deep to find.

In the end, Aeleth did not die. He vanished.

One morning, the guards found the throne empty, the scales resting gently upon it. No note. No sign. Just perfect stillness in the court, and the feeling that a line had been drawn.

Some say he became one with his artifact, that he was weighed by his own hand and found too heavy. Others say he watches still, somewhere in the spaces between dusk and dawn, ensuring that the balance does not tilt too far.

Thus ended the age of harmony. Light had passed. Balance had waned. Now, shadows stirred—and with them came the dark desires of those who followed.

**V. Susapeg of the Watching Eye**

*Fifth Emperor of Drynell — Bearer of the Eye of Apprehension*

The departure of Aeleth left a quiet tension in the empire, like the stillness before a storm. The courts were orderly, the provinces compliant—but the people felt something had shifted. The heart of the empire no longer beat with vision or virtue. What had been a living tree had become a machine of ticking wheels and measured shadows.

It was in this hour of unease that **Susapeg** rose—not by conquest, not by acclaim, but by consensus. A minister of impeccable appearance and calculated charm, he had served under Aeleth as Chief of Records. Some say Aeleth himself named Susapeg his heir before vanishing. Others whisper that Susapeg **forged the decree** and ensured no one lived to dispute it.

From the moment he donned the crown, Susapeg worried.

He feared rebellion, yet saw none. He feared betrayal, though none approached. Every praise felt like poison, every smile a mask. Day and night, he obsessed over the question: *What do they think of me?*

And so, he sought an answer not from his advisors or the stars, but from deeper, darker sources.

In secret, he commissioned the crafting of an artifact—the **Eye of Apprehension**—a black opal plated in gold and carved in the image of a serpent’s slit pupil. With grim resolve, he **cut out his own right eye** and placed the artifact in its place.

> “Let no veil remain between myself and their minds,” he said.\
> “If love cannot be trusted, let knowledge suffice.”

The **Eye**, once awakened, **granted him the power to see into thought**, to hear unspoken doubts, to peer past smiles and into the chambers of the soul. It did not lie. But it did not always tell the truth either—only the darkest truths it found.

The spirit of the Eye was said to be a **silver-scaled serpent**, always coiled at Susapeg’s feet, flicking its tongue toward each guest as they spoke. No one else saw it, but all felt its gaze.

Under Susapeg’s rule, **mirrors were hung in every hall**. Noble homes were required to install Imperial Listening Stones. His personal scribes compiled the **Codex of Hearts**, an ever-growing archive of thoughts, suspicions, and half-spoken dreams of the empire’s citizens. No law was passed without first reviewing the minds of those it might affect.

He did not torture. He did not murder. But still, a shadow fell across Drynell.

Fathers turned from sons. Lovers distrusted one another. No rebellion rose—but neither did loyalty. People did what they were told, not from respect, but fear of being misunderstood, misread, misjudged.

Even Susapeg’s own mind grew restless. The Eye revealed so many unspoken doubts, so many criticisms, that he began to wonder if anyone had ever loved him at all—or if he had only ever been a reflection of what others pretended to see.

> “Truth is a curse,” he wrote in his personal tome.\
> “But a beautiful one. I would rather burn with it than sleep in bliss.”

He died in his tower, surrounded by polished obsidian walls and no companions. His final command was that **the Eye be buried with him**, deep beneath the Palace of the Echoing Mind, locked behind seven riddles.

Some say it was. Others claim the Eye saw this coming—and arranged its own escape.

Thus ended the first of the dark emperors. And the people whispered: *“Better to be blind than watched. Better to be unknown than seen.”* But worse was still to come.

## **VI. Anzu of the Pendulums**

*Sixth Empress of Drynell — Bearer of the Ring of Seeking*

When the Eye was sealed in the tomb of the mind-obsessed emperor, the people hoped that peace might follow. But fear had seeped into the roots of the empire. Trust was rare, and hope—flickering. Into this uncertain age came **Anzu**, niece of Susapeg, the first woman in generations to claim the throne.

At first, there was joy. Anzu was bold, beautiful, and filled with passion. Her voice echoed like bells in the court. She spoke of **renewal**, of restoring not only the laws of Saulous and the mercy of Jaleth, but of lifting Drynell to heights even Metapo had not dreamed.

Yet behind her gleaming eyes was a gnawing hunger—a hunger for more. More glory. More knowledge. More treasure. More control over fate itself.

> “Why must we only rule the land,” she said, “when the secrets of the world lie beneath our feet, buried like bones in a forgotten field?”

She scoured the empire for the lost artifacts of her ancestors. The Pyramid, the Circlet, the Key—each was sought. Some she found shards of others, echoes. But none would bend to her hand.

So she made her own.

In the depths of her alchemical sanctum, aided by cryptic spirit-binders and relic-hunters, Anzu forged a new artifact: a **ring of obsidian and opal**, adorned with **five pendulums**, each carved from a different material—gold, pearl, ruby, obsidian, and bone. Each pendulum swung to the rhythm of a different desire:

- **Wealth**, to lead her to gold, tribute, and relics.

- **Power**, to show her those who opposed her reign.

- **Beauty**, to keep her ever radiant and beloved.

- **Knowledge**, to uncover hidden lore and magic.

- **Longevity**, to delay the grave and prolong her glory.

She called it the **Ring of Seeking**, and with it, **no secret could hide from her**.

The spirits bound to the ring were **five silver shadows**, formless but ever-watchful. They whispered in her ear constantly, each tempting her toward its own goal. They did not shout—but dripped like water on stone, ever insistent.

Soon, her rule transformed. Temples were stripped of their treasures, libraries pillaged for forbidden scrolls, tombs cracked open to extract the fragments of power interred within. Even the bones of Jaleth were taken from her grave, and her Key—long missing—was studied in the hope of deciphering its hidden magic.

Anzu was no tyrant like Ha-Des would be, but she was **relentless**. She hollowed the empire not by cruelty, but by **insatiability**. The nobles began to hoard their fortunes, afraid she would find and take them. The scholars ceased writing, afraid their thoughts would be harvested. The faithful fled to mountain chapels to hide from her gaze.

They say she no longer slept, for the pendulums ticked endlessly in her dreams. When she closed her eyes, she saw paths through the earth to treasures she could not reach. When she opened them, she saw only things she had yet to possess.

> “If I could find the first wish ever made,” she once said, “perhaps I could learn how to undo regret.”

In her final days, her beauty waned despite her efforts. Her mind fractured under the weight of so many desires unfulfilled. She wandered her palace, muttering riddles to herself, scratching maps on the walls with ink made of ash and wine.

She died not in grandeur, but in silence, her ring slipping from her hand and rolling beneath a crack in the stone. Some say it still ticks there, slowly, waiting for a hand desperate enough to seek it.

Thus fell the sixth of the Seven—**not to violence, nor rebellion, but to the insidious echo of always wanting more**.

And so the last of the line rose—darkest of them all. Not born to rule, but to dominate. Not remembered by name, but by shadow.

Certainly. Here is the final and most haunting chapter of the folk legend of the Seven Emperors of Drynell—**the tale of the Dark Ruler Ha-Des**, whose name was stricken from time and memory.

## **VII. The Dark Ruler Ha-Des**

*Seventh and Final Emperor of Drynell — Bearer of the Rod of Dominion*

> *“When the heart forgets virtue, power becomes hunger.\
> And when power forgets flesh, only shadow remains.”*

No one remembers the name he bore at birth. The last of the Seven Emperors has been erased from every tablet, every ledger, every corner of sanctioned memory. To speak his true name was once considered a treasonous curse. Now, he is known only as **Ha-Des**—*the Dark Ruler*, *the Unnamed One*, *the Spirit-Walker*.

His rise was quiet, veiled in the ashes of Anzu’s decline. Some say he was a minor lord of the Eastern borderlands, clever and soft-spoken. Others claim he was born of no mother, but conjured from the lingering darkness in the minds of the emperors before him. Wherever he came from, his ascent was swift. Charisma flowed from him like mist—cool, hypnotic, inescapable.

He found his artifact not in a ruin or tomb, but **in the Twilight Wastes**, a cursed land from the age of the Erosian Wars, where the ground is blackened and the air trembles with ancient sorrow. There, buried beneath a pillar of obsidian cracked by lightning, he uncovered a scepter: a **rod**, wrought of silver-veined onyx and wrapped in gold that hissed when touched.

When he took the **Rod of Dominion** into his hands, he did not hear voices.

He heard **silence**—and then a single word that echoed in his mind:

> “Submit.”

The Rod did not bend to its bearer. It **consumed him**, body and soul, forging him into a vessel for its will. He abandoned his human form, allowing his spirit to merge with shadow. His skin turned pale blue, his ears sharp, his eyes burning embers in a sea of ink. He became as the **Twili**, the long-banished creatures of shadow—sealed away after the fall of Erosia.

But where the Twili sought balance in shadow, Ha-Des sought **dominion**.

His rule was **not like the others**. He did not negotiate, judge, or persuade. He **invaded minds**, breaking will and memory alike. His army did not need banners—his enemies often turned upon their own kin, convinced in the night to serve the Dark Ruler’s cause.

He rebuilt the empire’s palaces not with gold, but **obsidian and echo-stone**—materials that reflected not the body, but the soul, so that all who approached saw what they feared most. His governors were mind-bound husks, speaking only when his will moved their tongues. The streets fell silent, the temples dimmed, and even the winds changed course to avoid his throne.

The **spirit of the Rod** was no beast or shade—it was **Ha-Des himself**, now more artifact than emperor. He did not sleep. He did not eat. He wandered his halls like a specter, passing through walls, whispering commands from nowhere. His mind existed in **a thousand others**, and no one knew which voice was truly his.

> “Your thoughts are mine,” he said. “Your dreams, my territory.”

But in time, even shadow devours itself.

The people, driven to desperation, rose—not in open rebellion, but in a **silent pact of defiance**. One by one, cities sealed themselves off. Priests scoured old rites. The dwarves and elves of the eastern mountains—those most rooted in ancient magics—sacrificed their greatest mages to cast a forbidden rite: **The Severing**.

And it worked. For one brief moment, the Rod flickered. His grip loosened. And in that fracture, his dominion collapsed.

Ha-Des **vanished**—some say into the wastes, others into the Rod itself, now hidden beyond mortal reach. Others believe he still wanders as a **spirit**, neither living nor dead, seeking a new bearer worthy—or foolish—enough to take up the Rod.

The Drynell Empire, shattered beyond repair, was no more.

# The Well of Nine Drops

(A traditional Hurlen Folk Tale)

Long ago, nestled deep within a hidden valley surrounded by towering mountains, there existed a well of unimaginable power. This well, known only in whispers as The Well of Nine Drops, could grant any wish—but only to those most in need. Many believed it was just a legend, for the valley itself was said to be impossible to find.

But one day, a weary traveler named Eli wandered the land. His clothes were tattered from years of journeying, and his feet were blistered from countless roads. His heart was heavy with sorrow, for he sought something he could not name—peace, perhaps, or a place to belong. The world had been harsh to him, and after years of wandering, his hope was all but gone.

One twilight evening, as the last rays of the sun dipped below the horizon, Eli stumbled upon a narrow path he had never noticed before. Drawn by a soft glow and a faint melody of running water, he followed it. The air grew sweet with the scent of blooming flowers, and the wind whispered secrets through the trees. The path led him to a valley bathed in silver moonlight, where a clear well stood in the center of a meadow.

The well shimmered as if the stars themselves had fallen into its depths. But it was not the well that caught Eli’s attention first—it was the rabbit.

A pure white rabbit sat atop the well’s stone rim, its fur glowing under the moon. Its eyes gleamed with wisdom far beyond its small form. The rabbit did not flee as Eli approached; instead, it spoke in a voice as soft as leaves brushing the forest floor.

“You have come far, traveler. The Well of Nine Drops reveals itself only to those whose need is true. But be warned—your wish will come to pass only if you drink exactly nine drops. No more, no less. Should you take the wrong amount, your wish will turn against you.”

Eli’s eyes widened, for he had heard tales of magical wells in his youth, but none as precise and mysterious as this.

“And what if I wish for something too great?” Eli asked, his voice hoarse.

“No wish is too great or too small,” the rabbit replied. “But every wish has a price.”

Eli stepped forward. The water glowed faintly, and as he gazed into its depths, he thought of all the things he could wish for: riches, power, fame. Yet none of those things stirred his heart.

He realized what he truly desired—rest, belonging, and a place where his weary soul could find peace.

With trembling hands, Eli cupped the water. The rabbit watched silently as he carefully counted each drop as it fell from his fingers into his mouth.

“One.”

“Two.”

“Three.”

He paused after each drop, steadying his breath.

“Four.”

“Five.”

“Six.”

The water tasted of spring rain and fresh dawns.

“Seven.”

“Eight.”

And finally—

“Nine.”

As the ninth drop touched his tongue, the well shimmered with blinding light. The rabbit leapt from the rim and vanished into the shadows.

The valley around Eli transformed. Trees blossomed with golden fruit, birds sang sweet songs, and a small cottage appeared by the well—a place warm and welcoming, built of stone and wood, as if it had always been waiting for him.

Eli felt the weight of his years lift. His tired bones grew strong, and his heart light. He had found his home—a place where he belonged, where he would never wander again.

From that day onward, the Well of Nine Drops remained hidden, waiting patiently for the next soul most in need. And some say that if you wander far and long enough, with a wish deep in your heart, you might find the valley under silver moonlight.

But only if the white rabbit deems you worthy.

# The Shattering and the Severing

A Tale of the Hurlen War

In the three-hundredth year of the Age of Concord, peace blanketed the continent of Hurly like the last snow of spring—fragile, fleeting, and illusory. For deep in the silver-cloaked forests of Hossari, a boy named **Gillian Gearson**, whose mind had been twisted by forest spirits and grief, unwittingly became the herald of calamity.

Driven by voices not her own, he uncovered an ancient seal buried in the ruins of a lost city of Hossari—once a city of dreams, now long reclaimed by vine and root. With hands slick from rust and magic, he broke the seal. And from the twilight maw rose **Erosia**, Twin of the Darkness, long ago banished to the shadow realm for her rebellion against her twin, **Quinn**, Lord of Day.

The seal shattered—and with it, the wall between realms. The Twili came first: eyes aglow with dusklight, clad in shadows made flesh. Then came the **Husks**, cursed remnants of the dead, twisted into servitude by the **Husk Queen**, their leader forged in agony. Hossari was the first to fall.

Yet this was not the only storm to stir.

On the southern shores, the **Kobold Empire**, guided by Empress Joan and her council of Metabold warlords—earthshapers empowered by ancient **snawk venom**—launched a mighty invasion. Backed by the fractured southern kingdoms and radical Rokesh cultists, they sought to expand their empire. The banner of Rokesh flew from the towers of Alachua and Pomera, and Kobold war-machines rolled northward, cracking the stones of the old Alliance roads.

Thus began the Tri-War—a three-headed fire:\
The **Federation of Hurley**, noble yet divided, rallied behind the frozen hills of **Rus**.\
The **Twili**, led by Erosia, sought to remake the world in endless dusk.\
The **Kobold Empire**, armored in stone and guided by divine love, marched for dominion.

The world wept. Cities like Nabatea and Heraklion shook beneath cannon and claw. Fields once golden were turned red, and no prayer rose unanswered—save those for peace.

But in Hurley’s darkest hour, hope stirred.

A party of champions rose from ash and ruin

With them walked the last hope of the divine dragons: the **New Draconic Champions**, children born not of blood, but of the six primal magics. These champions, as foretold by the ancient prophecy of Quint, were each destined to reflect the virtues of the original six dragons.

In a final confrontation beneath the roots of **Yggdrasil**, deep within the Lake of Mystery, the Avengers confronted **Erosia**. There, the Dragon of Night beheld her creation—chaos, suffering, shadow—and through the plea of the champions and the song of the heroes, **she remembered love**.

Erosia wept, and her sorrow unraveled the spell that bound the Twili and the Husks. Their armies dissolved like mist at dawn.

But peace had its price.

With Erosia’s fall, the balance shifted. The Federation, broken and weakened by its war with the Twili, could no longer resist the might of the Kobold war machine. Though the Empress Joan’s heart was heavy, her legions claimed victory. The **Treaty of Silver Ash** was signed in Hyperion beneath the broken dome of the Hall of Unity.

Hurley was not conquered.\
Hurley was *absorbed*.

Now, the continent remains a shadow of its former self—**a collection of client states**, each bowing in some way to the Kobold Empire. The Federation lives on, not in defiance, but as **a memory with flags**. Erosia wanders the world in penance, her form mortal, her power sealed.

# The Countries of Hurley (Post-Tri-War Era Revision)

## Introduction:

The following document provides a geopolitical snapshot of the continent of Hurly in the aftermath of two cataclysmic conflicts: the Great Hurlen War and the Tri-War. Once a land of proud kingdoms and independent federations, Hurly now finds itself largely subdued and fragmented under the dominion of the Kobold Empire, also known as the Kledgelony.

Three years have passed since the end of the Tri-War—a brutal, three-front struggle between:

- The Kobold Empire, whose snawk-empowered metabolds and divine mandate from Rokesh laid waste to entire coalitions.

- The Twilight Throne, led by Erosia and her Twili and Husk legions, emerging from the Twilight Realm through shattered seals and ancient rites.

- The remnants of the Hurlen Federation, primarily based in the frozen bastion of Rus, struggling to hold together scattered alliances in the north.

Though Erosia was ultimately subdued—her armies dissolved and her form bound in mortal penance—the cost to the Federation was total. The signing of the Treaty of Silver Ash in the broken Hall of Unity at Hyperion marked the official end of Hurley’s independence. From this shattered peace rose a new political order.

Today, nearly every state on the continent exists as a protectorate of the Kobold Empire, whether through direct governance, puppet rulers, or military occupation. The once-proud flags of the Federation fly now only in ceremony, a hollow echo of sovereignty. Even Hyperion, the Eternal City, now serves more as a cultural showcase for Imperial diplomacy than a symbol of free peoples.

This document catalogues the current political, geographical, and historical states of the nations of Hurly as they exist under this new, imperial regime.

- Kobold loyalists praise the infrastructure improvements and economic aid from Korland.

- Twili cultural loyalists, especially in the south, maintain small “Still Circles” that keep alive Erosia’s vision in hidden groves and former temples.

- Corsican nationalists, both old-guard monarchists and younger rebels, chafe at the foreign presence—whether it be stone-scaled or shadow-veiled.

A resistance movement, the Circle of the Unseen Dawn, is rumored to be forming in the ruins of the White River Pass. Their symbol—a crown of starlight behind a broken anvil—has begun appearing in burned fields and on garrison walls.

### Current Status: Province of Partnership and Promise

Ete today stands as a beacon of peaceful transformation, celebrated by the Empire as proof that unity need not mean erasure. In fact, many now refer to the nation as the "Jewel of Accord"—a rare place where magic, machinery, and mutual respect converge.

> “If conquest is the only path, we shall innovate conquest itself—into collaboration.”\

### During the War: The Crumbling Shield

- The Siege of Aronia, the capital, lasted three months and left much of the upper city in ruin.

- Its armies, under-equipped and outmatched, were forced into conscription drafts, draining rural villages to feed the front lines.

- Ultimately, King Elogio Napolei, desperate to preserve his throne and legacy, signed the Treaty of Ashen Dawn—ceding Napolei to the Kobold Empire in exchange for personal protection and ceremonial status.

The betrayal shocked the people—many of whom had given everything to resist the very empire that now claimed them. But the story didn’t end in bitterness.

### Post-War Role: From Betrayal to Blueprint

What followed was one of the most dramatic transformations in Hurly.

The Kobold administration, seeking to prove that conquest could yield prosperity, poured unprecedented resources into Napolei:

- The Reclamation Corps rebuilt Aronia from its ruins, introducing geomantic sewerworks, clean water, and paved roads within five years.

- Kobold and Etian scholars launched the Napoleian School Reform Act, which introduced public literacy, agricultural alchemy, and basic civic training.

- The countryside saw a redistribution of farmland, and the hereditary tax systems that once enriched nobles at the peasants’ expense were abolished.

Today, Napolei is considered the "Model Protectorate"—a propaganda jewel for the Empire and a living case study in successful integration.

### Current Status: Loyal, Lifted, and Watched

While King Elogio remains on the throne, he is widely disrespected and effectively irrelevant. Real power lies with Minister-Regent Mireille du Fable, a Kobold bureaucrat with a reputation for stern efficiency and unexpected compassion.

- Napolei boasts the highest approval rating of imperial governance among all protectorates.

- A new merchant class, largely Kobold-trained, has begun revitalizing trade networks once lost to war.

- Former rebels and war orphans now serve proudly in the Imperial Civil Guard, wearing stone-and-gold uniforms and reciting oaths to Rokesh and the Rule of Renewal.

Yet not all are content. A small faction of ex-nobles and veterans, calling themselves the Flame of Aronia, continues to agitate for the restoration of true monarchy—though their numbers dwindle.

> New Napoleian Motto:\
> *"We fought. We fell. We rose again—stronger than stone."*

## Name: The Papal State (The Holy See of Phantos, Ecclesiastical Protectorate)

Former Title: The Papacy (The Isekai’ed Holy See)\
Current Government: *Elected Theocratic Monarchy* with guaranteed religious autonomy\
Ruler: Pope-Vicarate Victor I\
Imperial Authority: Religious affairs acknowledged but not administered by the Imperial Ministry of Cultural Integrity, headquartered in Heraklion

### Geography:

The Papal State lies nestled between Plana to the north, Kambot to the east, Lakose and the River of Fate to the south, and Ete to the west. Its geography includes fertile northern plains and the spiritually significant Southern Green, a dense forest that houses numerous shrines, monasteries, and quiet cloisters.

### During the War: Resistance Through Faith

At the war’s outbreak, the Papal State mounted staunch resistance, fearing not just political domination, but spiritual dilution. Armed with faith and driven by zeal, the Palatine Guard and several rural militias held off Kobold advances for months.

However, after the burning of the Shrine of Surculus and heavy civilian casualties in the siege of Laetare, Pope-Vicarate Victor I entered into direct negotiations with the Kobold High Command.

The resulting Accord of the Twelve Branches ensured:

- The Papacy would surrender military sovereignty, entering the Kobold imperial system as a protectorate.

- In return, the Kobold Empire would guarantee the Church’s full religious freedom, including freedom of worship, education, and internal governance.

- The Church would not be required to syncretize or modify doctrine to align with Kobold beliefs.

### Post-War Role: Peaceful Persistence

While the Kobolds did not adopt the Catholic faith or promote its expansion, they have tolerated it with respectful distance:

- Churches and monasteries remain untouched, and pilgrims are permitted to travel freely.

- Catholic institutions retain full autonomy over education and liturgy, but are expected to remain politically neutral.

- Missionary efforts are permitted, but met with indifference or polite resistance in most Kobold-majority territories.

Some minor conversions have occurred—particularly among human vassals and rural Kobolds intrigued by liturgical aesthetics or moral teachings—but these are isolated and not institutionally encouraged.

### Current Status: Quiet Strength

The Papal State today stands as a modestly prosperous protectorate, focused on internal consolidation and spiritual outreach:

- Pope-Vicarate Victor I is respected as a moral voice but has little influence outside Papal borders.

- The Basilica of the Twelve Branches remains a sacred destination, but the Empire offers no formal recognition of its status.

- Catholic clergy have been granted travel privileges, but imperial bureaus do not consult them on legal or ethical matters.

Though the Church no longer fears eradication, it also knows its position is permitted, not privileged.

> Papal Motto in the post-war era:\
> *“We do not rule. We do not rebel. We remain.”*

## Name: Plana (The Planan Nomadlands, Semi-Autonomous March of the Empire)

Former Title: The Planan Nomads\
Current Government: *Council of Tribes* under Imperial Treaty of Recognition\
Ruler: Chief Yegu af Teleboge (First Among Equals)\
Imperial Authority: Advised by the Marchwarden of the Open East, stationed in the capital-camp of Tel Ornon

### Geography:

Plana stretches across the Great Plains of southeastern Hurly, bordered by Corsica to the north, Zorat to the east, Kambot and the Papal States to the south, and Ishmakale to the west. Its landscape is vast, open, and ever-changing—rolling grasslands, hidden ravines, and ancient stone totems marking the paths of wandering herds and the memories of fallen chiefs.

### During the War: The War with No Lines

Plana became a war without borders during the Tri-War. Its plains were impossible to fortify, and the shifting nature of Planan society made it impossible to occupy in any traditional sense.

- The Twili established mist-camps and spiritual pylons, only to have them sabotaged or bypassed within days.

- Kobold siege caravans struggled to maintain traction on open ground, facing hit-and-run tactics from mounted Planan lancers and wolf-mounted scouts.

- The Guerilla Chiefs of the Three Winds, led by Yegu af Teleboge, waged a decentralized campaign to preserve tribal independence and protect sacred grazing routes.

Plana’s people fought not for ideology, but for land, memory, and the right to roam.

### Post-War Role: The March That Cannot Be Mapped

After the war, the Kobold Empire—recognizing both the futility of occupation and the value of cooperation—negotiated the Treaty of Ornon, establishing Plana as a semi-autonomous march.

Key terms included:

- Recognition of the Council of Tribes as the legitimate governing body of Planan affairs.

- The placement of a single Kobold emissary, the Marchwarden, tasked not with ruling, but mediating disputes, protecting trade routes, and ensuring compliance with imperial law.

- The Planan right to maintain open migration, traditional grazing, and seasonal tribal festivals without interference.

In return, the Planan tribes agreed to:

- Escort imperial envoys and caravans across the plains.

- Share strategic intelligence on Zorat and any remaining Twili incursions.

- Allow the Empire to rebuild and maintain ancient roads and waystones, now marked with both tribal and Kobold symbols.

### Current Status: Roaming, Watching, Remembering

Today, Plana remains a land in motion:

- Chief Yegu af Teleboge, old but unbroken, is revered as a living legend—one who fought all three sides and still walks free.

- The Great Hoof Festival has resumed, drawing both Planan clans and curious outsiders to witness ritual horse-dances, song-combats, and open-air debates.

- Kobold diplomats stationed in Tel Ornon live in mobile stone-yurts, built with geomantic foundations that slide with the shifting camps.

Tensions remain between some younger warriors and the more pragmatic elders, but most tribes see the Empire’s presence not as tyranny—but as another wind to ride, so long as it blows gently.

> Planan Saying:\
> *“The land has no king. Only the sky tells us where to sleep.”*

## Name: Pomera (The People's Republic of Pomera, Imperial Protectorate in Rebellion)

Former Title: The People's Republic of Pomera\
Current Government: *Imperial-Installed Consulate* under contested rule\
Nominal Rulers: Consuls Arturo Valdovinos and Luka Fernández (Imperial appointees)\
Imperial Authority: Administered by the Office of Civil Reintegration, heavily monitored by the Imperial Internal Security Corps

### Geography:

Pomera is located in the lush and temperate southeast of Hurly, bordered by Mongues and Alachua to the west, Lakose to the east, the Bay of Bliss to the south, and Heraklion to the north. Its fertile farmlands and coastal fisheries made it a strategic food and cash crop supplier, especially during wartime, when its cotton and tobacco exports kept armies clothed and coffers filled.

### During the War: The Misplaced Hope

Pomera was long a thorn in the Federation's side—independent in spirit, collectivist in law, and revolutionary in culture. When the war broke out, they saw the Kobolds not as conquerors, but as potential liberators from Ishmakale's centralist rule.

- Pomera entered the war as allies to the Kobold Empire, signing the Covenant of Red Grain, a treaty that promised autonomy, sovereignty, and a mutual trade pact after the fall of the Federation.

- Their militias—particularly the Red Shard Brigades—fought side-by-side with Kobold legions against Ismakale and Celeste.

- But as the war ended, the Covenant was quietly dissolved, and the Kobolds installed Consuls Valdovinos and Fernández, figures seen by the people as imperial collaborators.

The betrayal cut deep. Pomera had not been conquered—they had been used.

### Post-War Role: The Protectorate That Refuses to Bow

Unlike other protectorates, Pomera remains openly hostile to imperial presence:

- Imperial banners are routinely defaced or torn down in rural districts.

- The People’s Assembly, a revolutionary democratic council outlawed by the Empire, still meets in secret across countryside estates.

- The Red Shard Remnants, a guerrilla resistance movement, actively targets supply caravans, informants, and outposts—particularly in the Bayline provinces.

Despite these acts of defiance, the Kobolds have not yet launched a full crackdown, fearing it would ignite a continent-wide rebellion. Instead, they rely on:

- Tactical propaganda, painting the Consuls as reformers and portraying dissent as “anti-people, not anti-empire.”

- Economic pressure, redirecting key exports through Napolei and Mongues to cut Pomera out of the supply chain.

- Deployment of the Imperial Internal Security Corps, a shadowy agency that monitors speech, education, and spiritual movements.

### Current Status: On a Razor’s Edge

Pomera today is a nation boiling under the lid:

- In cities like Vallacar and New Bellatora, strikes, street preachers, and smuggled pamphlets speak of renewal, uprising, and red dawns.

- The countryside, especially along the Fallow Crescent, is under near-martial law, with Kobold patrols frequently ambushed and repelled.

- A new revolutionary symbol—the Open Hand Clutching Flame—has begun appearing on barn doors, alley walls, and broken monuments.

Though still officially a protectorate, many Kobolds refer to Pomera not as a subject, but as a containment zone.

> Pomeran Saying (banned under imperial law):\
> *“We were not conquered. We were courted, lied to, and awakened.”*

## Name: Rus (The Kingdom of Rus, Imperial Protectorate of the North)

Former Title: The Rus Kingdom\
Current Government: *Autonomous Royal Protectorate* under Imperial Concord\
Ruler: King Bors IX\
Imperial Authority: Respected liaison via the Imperial Northern Accord Council, based in Fuchsgate

### Geography:

Rus dominates the frozen northeastern mountains of Hurly, bordered by Fuchs to the southwest and the Silver Sea and Coastal Sea to the north and east. The Rusan Mountains, rich in volcanic activity, have long sustained Rus through advanced metallurgy, geothermal forges, and ancient runic crafting. Harsh, insular, and resilient, Rus is often called “The Frozen Forge.”

### During the War: The Nation That Never Yielded

Rus stood tall and untouched throughout the entirety of the Tri-War. Its natural defenses and harsh climate made it nearly impossible to invade, but its true shield was Fuchs—the Order forged to hold back any who dared approach the mountain passes.

- Rus sent no soldiers to the front. It supplied only weapons, armor, and sacred runes—crafted in silence, traded at great cost.

- Its elite blacksmiths and rune-carvers ensured that both Federation loyalists and Fuchsian knights could resist longer than expected.

- Kobold scouts reached the outer slopes once—but never advanced beyond the Stone Line, where volcanic wards and blizzard spirits guard the high passes.

While others fought and fell, Rus endured—proud and patient.

### Post-War Role: The Crown That Chose to Kneel

After the fall of the Federation and the rise of Heraklion, Rus faced a world utterly changed. Surrounded by imperial protectorates, even its stone-hard isolation could not last forever.

Rather than resist the inevitable, King Bors IX made a calculated decision:

- He signed the Accord of Ash and Frost, formally entering Rus into the Kobold Empire’s protectorate system.

- The treaty ensured complete internal sovereignty, guaranteed protection for Fuchs, and imperial non-interference in all sacred metallurgical and runic traditions.

- In return, Rus agreed to supply imperial command structures with arms, enchanted steel, and advanced geothermal forging techniques.

The decision was met with grim approval by the Rus people. “Better to bend a knee in strength than be broken while standing,” one elder said.

### Current Status: Silent, Sovereign, and Watching

Today, Rus remains one of the most autonomous protectorates within the Empire:

- Its mountain halls are untouched, and Heraklion officials may enter only by invitation.

- Fuchs continues to serve as its shield-wall, now reinforced with imperial-supplied arms but governed solely by the Order.

- The Cathedral-Forges of Brak Zarn and the Runewells of Gadrask continue to produce legendary arms sought after by Kobolds and Twili alike.

Though not openly rebellious, Rus is watched carefully by the Empire—and Rus watches back.

> Rus Saying:\
> *“We were not taken. We were not spared. We chose our time—and shaped our chains.”*

## Name: Swedanetil (The Kingdom of Swedanetil, Twilight Sanctuary-Protectorate)

Former Title: The Kingdom of Swedanetil\
Current Government: *Royal Protectorate* with integrated Twili cultural authority\
Ruler: Queen-Regent Aibhilín Uí Maonghaile\
Imperial Authority: Jointly managed by the Imperial Ministry of Integration and the Twilight Embassy of the North

### Geography:

Swedanetil lies on the northwestern coast of Hurly, bordered by the Silver Sea to the north, Hossari to the east, Celeste to the southeast, Monteforte to the south, and the Slaver’s Sea to the west. Its lands include verdant forests, mountain-fed rivers, and misty coastlines dotted with lighthouses and pre-Federation temple ruins.

### During the War: The Peaceful Conquest

Swedanetil was among the first nations to fall to the Twili, not through fire or blood, but through silence and song. In the opening weeks of the Tri-War:

- The Twili shadow-legions descended through the Northern Mountain Passes, bypassing Celeste entirely.

- Swedanetil’s military, unprepared for dream-magic and soulbinding tactics, offered little resistance.

- Queen-Regent Aibhilín surrendered in exchange for the preservation of her people and the guarantee of cultural autonomy.

Twili occupation was orderly, spiritual, and restrained. The native Swedanetil people adapted to the rhythms of dusk-born ritual, and many came to appreciate the grace and gentleness of their new neighbors.

Swedanetil became a logistics and healing zone, supporting the Twili efforts on the Montefortian front with food, mystics, and sanctuary hospitals. The land was never again touched by siege or blade.

### Post-War Role: The Twilight’s Quiet Refuge

After Erosia’s penance and the dissolution of the Twilight Throne, the Twili were scattered and vulnerable. Rather than exile them again, the Kobold Empire brokered an accord:

- Swedanetil was declared a Twilight Sanctuary-Protectorate, allowing Twili refugees, artists, and priests to settle in its forests, coastlands, and high glades.

- The Queen-Regent retained her throne, but now governs in tandem with the Twilight Embassy of the North, led by the soft-spoken dream-lord Telmiren Lur.

- The Kobolds oversee the territory from afar, trusting in its balance and strategic irrelevance.

Swedanetil is now home to:

- Twilight cloisters, where dreamweaving and memory-craft are practiced openly.

- Shared festivals, where Swedanetil nobles and Twili mystics perform combined rituals of light, shadow, and sea-wind.

### Current Status: Graceful, Softly Divided

Swedanetil is a land of eerie calm and cultural convergence:

- The native population has largely embraced the Twili, though some older nobles bristle at their influence over governance and spiritual rites.

- The Twili, in turn, see Swedanetil as a gift from Erosia’s sorrow—a place to mourn, remember, and renew.

- A fringe sect known as the Veilkeepers has begun blending Twili philosophy with Rokeshite stone-spirituality, seeking a unified twilight theology.

The Kobold Empire regards Swedanetil as a low-priority zone—stable, silent, and unlikely to rebel. But they watch it still, mindful that from silence, history often stirs.

> Twili Proverb now etched into the Sea-Gate of Swedanetil:\
> *“Here, the sun does not set. It sighs.”*

## Name: Utrecht (The Province of Utrecht, Imperial Frontier Protectorate)

Former Title: The Kingdom of Utrecht\
Current Government: *Imperial Administrative Territory* under civilian-military oversight\
Nominal Leader: Governor Almarek of the High Valley (Imperial-Appointed Marshal-Governor)\
Imperial Authority: Managed by the Office of Frontier Stabilization, based in Heraklion with a local seat in Bragholt

### Geography:

Utrecht lies along the high eastern ranges and low river valleys between Fuchs to the north and east, Napolei to the south, and Hossari to the west. A land of mist-laden ravines, frostbitten ridges, and ruin-laced border forests, Utrecht’s terrain is treacherous, beautiful, and brutal—an ideal ground for guerrilla warfare and prolonged resistance.

### During the War: Broken but Unyielding

At the start of the Tri-War, Utrecht resisted both Twili and Kobold advances, holding out longer than most neighboring states. Its mountain militias, known as the Winter Pike Companies, used terrain, hidden paths, and insurgent tactics to repel early offensives.

But after a year of pressure, Utrecht fell to the Twili, who:

- Converted key temples and court-houses into shadow-cloisters and memory sanctums.

- Imposed curfews enforced by Huskwalkers, sparking mass uprisings.

- Executed dozens of community leaders, fueling deep hatred and vengeance oaths.

Throughout occupation, rebel cells such as the Pale Lantern, Sons of Bragholt, and Valewatch conducted sabotage, assassinations, and information smuggling in coordination with Fuchs and Rus.

### Post-War Role: Cold Peace under Stone Hands

After the war, the Kobold Empire moved swiftly to dismantle the Twili apparatus and reassert control:

- The Pact of Emberlight, signed in the ruins of Bragholt, allowed Utrecht to enter the protectorate system in exchange for the removal of all remaining Twili influence.

- Kobold metabold patrols, backed by local fighters, rooted out Twilight sanctums still clinging to the ravines.

- Utrecht was reorganized into a frontier protectorate, with direct Imperial oversight but significant local input in military and infrastructure decisions.

Today, Utrecht is a tense but functioning territory:

- Governor Almarek, a former rebel commander turned imperial official, governs as a military marshal, balancing Kobold expectations with folk justice and loyalty networks.

- Infrastructure is being rebuilt—roads carved with Kobold runes, watchtowers built from stone and steel, and a new militia academy established in Bragholt.

- Twili symbols and rites remain outlawed—possession of dusk-weave or memory scrolls is punishable by exile.

### Current Status: Sober, Scarred, and Vigilant

Utrecht today is a land of cautious acceptance:

- Most citizens prefer Kobold stability over the memory-haunted rule of the Twili.

- However, veterans of the rebellion remain influential, and the Empire walks carefully to avoid sparking new uprisings.

- The Reconstruction Guilds, part of the Kobold civil apparatus, are largely respected, especially for clearing mines, restoring bridges, and building public halls.

In the high valleys, people still whisper old songs, but they no longer carry blades when they sing.

> Utrechter Saying:\
> *“We lost to shadow. We bowed to stone. But we remember who we are.”*

## Name: Xeries (The Principality of Xeries, Imperial Resource Protectorate)

Imperial Authority: Coordinated by the Imperial Ministry of Resource Oversight, with a station in Elderglen

Xeries lies in the southeastern woods of Hurly, nestled between Lakose to the north, Pomera to the west, and the Coastal Sea to the south and east. The Great Eastern Greenwood, also known as the Crescent Canopy, defines its geography—vast, misted forests filled with timber, rare herbs, and whispering spirits.

### During the War: Peace Through Partnership

Xeries was among the first to formally align with the Kobold Empire, signing the Treaty of Bark and Stone mere weeks after the Empire’s forces began their continental campaign.

- Princess Yalara IX, citing the nation's limited military and deeply spiritual connection to nature, sought to protect her forests and people through alliance rather than resistance.

- In return for sovereignty and spiritual protection, Xeries agreed to become a primary lumber supplier, harvesting and refining the dense elderwood and flame-resistant pine needed for siege engines, shipbuilding, and fortifications.

While other nations burned or bled, Xeries remained untouched by invasion, serving instead as a quiet logistics lifeline for the Kobolds throughout the war.

### Post-War Role: The Empire’s Greenwood

After the war, Xeries maintained its protectorate status as a key natural resource partner:

- Timber quotas are determined annually by the Imperial Ministry of Resource Oversight, with strict ecological preservation clauses in place (honored more faithfully in Xeries than elsewhere).

- Princess Yalara IX continues to rule with quiet grace, beloved by her people for keeping the country safe and spiritually intact.

Kobold officials in Xeries are seen less as occupiers and more as accountants, ecologists, and trade partners—though some rural communities still resent the standardized harvest schedules and road expansions.

### Current Status: Quiet Prosperity, Watchful Growth

Xeries today is a land of serene stability:

- Its druidic circles, though carefully monitored, retain legal authority over sacred groves.

- The Forestguard, Xeries’ elite ranger company, now trains with Kobold terramancers to track illegal poachers and protect glade sanctuaries.

- A new class of merchant-rangers has arisen, bridging Kobold trade law with traditional Xerian ecology.

Though content, some worry that imperial reliance on Xerian wood will only increase—and with it, pressure to allow deeper cuts into protected forest zones.

> Xerian Proverb:\

## Name: Zorat (The Territory of Zorat, Imperial Pacification Zone)

Former Title: The Warchiefdom of Zorat\
Current Government: *Direct Military Governance* under Imperial Martial Law\
Nominal Ruler: None — governed by Marshal-Governor Fraxin du Rochelaine, under mandate from Heraklion\
Imperial Authority: The Imperial Directorate of Internal Security and Stabilization maintains sole oversight

### Geography:

Zorat lies in the eastern lowlands of Hurly, bordered by Plana to the west, Utrecht to the north, Kambot and the Papal States to the south, and the Mountains of Rus to the northeast. It is a land of dense thickets, red clay plains, and scorched highlands, where long-forgotten ruins and crude fortress-towns dot the landscape.

### During the War: The Last Roar of the Federation

Zorat, long known for its unruly goblin-led tribal federations and warlord aristocracies, was among the final territories of the Federation to fall, holding out even after the fall of Hyperion and Celeste.

- Its infamous Ironspike Council, a loose assembly of tribal chieftains and war-generals, refused surrender—launching raids deep into Kobold-occupied territory from concealed earthworks and marshlands.

- Zorat was a thorn in the side of both Twili and Kobold forces, often switching sides mid-campaign and defying all expectations of coordinated governance.

- The final siege, known as the Ash Hunt, took three months and cost the Kobolds thousands of troops, largely due to the guerilla tactics and savage terrain.

When it finally fell, the Ironspike Council was executed, and the region was placed under permanent martial governance.

### Post-War Role: A Region Still at War

Officially, Zorat is a protectorate. In truth, it is a pacification zone—an occupied territory not yet civilized in the eyes of the Empire:

- Marshal-Governor Fraxin du Rochelaine, a metabold renowned for his ruthlessness, governs from Fort Rek’tar, an obsidian bastion raised over the former Ironspike Council hall.

- The region has been divided into resource zones and military districts, patrolled by Metabold legions and Kobold-trained dog-riders.

- Public gatherings are banned. Tribal customs are outlawed. Imperial law is enforced with stone and fire.

Despite this, the goblins of Zorat remain unbroken:

- Scattered remnants of the Ashfang Clans, once loyal to the Ironspike Council, now operate as mobile raiding bands, targeting supply lines, mining caravans, and reconstruction crews.

- These goblin warbands have adapted—learning Kobold tactics, stealing imperial equipment, and even hiring rogue Celestian mercenaries.

- A mysterious war-leader known only as “Red Scythe” is said to unite several clans in secret, aiming not to restore Zorat's government, but to burn imperial outposts one by one.

### Current Status: Suppressed but Smoldering

Zorat today is the Empire’s deepest wound:

- Trade caravans pass only under heavy escort.

- Most Kobolds posted to Zorat do not return, or return changed.

- Reconstruction is minimal, limited to fortified depots and watchtowers.

Even among loyal Kobold citizens, Zorat is spoken of in hushed tones—a place where the Empire may have won the war, but not the land.

> Saying etched into a Kobold helmet found outside Fort Rek’tar:\
> *“You can crush the stone, but you cannot tame the fire beneath it.”*
