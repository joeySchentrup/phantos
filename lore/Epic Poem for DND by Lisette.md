## Canto I – Metapo the Fortunate Flame

- When Hurley wept ‘neath banners torn,

  And war was all the land had worn,

  A wind-blown child from western sands

  Rose up with fire cupped in his hands.

  Metapo, crowned in comet’s breath,

  Was born where stars entwined with death.

  The shattered tree, its roots laid bare,

  Did cradle gold beyond compare—

  A Pyramid, not made by men,

  It whispered once, and spoke again.

  It danced in dreams, it shaped his fate,

  It led him through the jealous gate.

  Fortune kissed his every stride,

  And kings did kneel, and traitors died.

  Yet prideful peaks, they would not bend—

  One mountain kept him from the end.

  His flame grew dim, the song grew soft,

  His banner torn, but still aloft.

  Though unity he near obtained,

  It was the song—not crown—he gained.

## Canto II – Saulous the Thoughtful Son

- Where flame once roared, now silence stayed.

  Metapo gone, the pyres decayed.

  From silvered domes where shadows curled,

  Young Saulous saw beyond the world.

  A circlet, ancient, star-thought made,

  Brought light where none before had strayed.

  It wrapped his brow in silent grace—

  He saw not just the time and place,

  But what could be beyond the flame,

  Beyond the pride, beyond the name.

  He gathered not by blade or spear,

  But by the trust that tames the fear.

  Two owls above—of night and noon—

  Marked him wise, a tempered boon.

  And where his father’s fire did fail,

  His quiet thought became the gale.

  Treaties rose like towers tall,

  And Hurley bowed to wisdom’s call.

## Canto III – Jaleth of the Kind Key

- Then Jaleth came with smile serene,

  An empress born where dreams had been.

  Beside the Lake of Mystery’s shore,

  Where spirits dance and lost hearts soar,

  She found the Key of Concord bright,

  Its surface humming peace and light.

  With touch alone, she broke men’s chains,

  And whispered balm on battle’s pains.

  No sword she bore, yet foes grew mild—

  She turned the butcher to a child.

  Her rule was long, her gardens wide,

  No locks remained she had not tried.

  Drynell bloomed beneath her reign,

  Its rivers free from blood or chain.

  Though time did pass, her echo stayed—

  A lullaby the silence made.

## Canto IV – Aeleth the Keeper of Scales

- But time, relentless, brought the weight

  Of balance, law, and tempered fate.

  Aeleth rose with quiet might,

  The sun on left, moon on his right.

  The Scales of Equinox he bore,

  A gift from gods, or something more.

  He judged not fast, nor judged in vain,

  But weighed each soul through joy and pain.

  He built great halls where truth was tried,

  Where lies met light, and none could hide.

  Yet cold is balance, sharp and bare—

  The heart grows weary of what’s fair.

  Still, kingdoms stood beneath his sway,

  Though warmth and cheer had fled away.

  His was the reign of silent peace,

  That wanes not quick—but knows its cease.

## Canto V – Susapeg of the Watching Eye

- Then crept the age of fear and glass,

  Where every truth wore veil or mask.

  Susapeg came, of noble name,

  But mind devoured by secret flame.

  He tore his eye from socket red,

  And in its place, the Eye of Dread.

  It saw through lies, through skin and thought—

  But left his soul in shadow caught.

  His mirrors watched, his halls were deep,

  Where none could dream, and none could sleep.

  He ruled by doubt, by whisper’s weight,

  And crumbled all he sought to sate.

  A thousand truths he found each day—

  But not the one to guide his way.

  And so the owl and lion fled,

  And Hurley knelt to fear instead.

## Canto VI – Anzu of the Pendulums

- Then Anzu came, the hungering mind,

  With hands of theft and eyes unkind.

  She forged a Ring of fivefold pull—

  Each pendulum, a hunger full:

  For wealth, for power, beauty, lore,

  And life that death could touch no more.

  She wanted all, and thus began

  To raze the roots of god and man.

  The tombs she robbed, the books she broke,

  The spirits bound with iron yoke.

  She cracked the Key, unwove its song,

  To learn why right might turn from wrong.

  But each desire deeper grew,

  And all she had turned cold, untrue.

  Her court was ghosts, her throne—a shell,

  A market made from dreams that fell.

## Canto VII – Ha-Des, the Dark Ruler

- And then—the silence split in dread.

  A name unsaid, a godless thread.

  Ha-Des, they call him—none know why,

  But say he once looked to the sky.

  He found the Rod where dusk had grown,

  And bled himself to claim its throne.

  It took his form, his name, his will—

  And gave him back a god to kill.

  He did not ask, he did not reign—

  He was, and all else broke in pain.

  The minds he stole, the hearts he cracked,

  The saints that wept with faces blacked.

  The Twili walked with chains of breath,

  And called him master, doom, and death.

  The world took flame, and Drynell fell—

  A funeral beneath a spell.

## Canto VIII – The End and the Echo

- Now broken lies the storied crown,

  And Hurley’s stars no longer drown.

  The Artifacts, once bright with pride,

  Are scattered, sealed, or turned to tide.

  Yet still the whispers ride the air,

  And still the roots remember care.

  “Seven crowns for seven hands,

  Seven ghosts in seven lands.

  One shall rise, and one shall fall—

  When hearts forget, they lose it all.”

  So mark the flame, the frost, the key,

  The scales, the eye, the hungers three—

  And mark the Rod, where shadows lie.

  For from the ash, a spark may fly.

  And should the winds of fate entwine,

  The song shall rise—a final time.

  Fin

  A tale of rise, of fall, of flame—

  And seven echoes without name.
