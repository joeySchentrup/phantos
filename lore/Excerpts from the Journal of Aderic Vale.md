*The following passages are traditionally attributed to Aderic Vale, an adventurer who lived in the distant antiquity of Hurly. Later copies identify the unnamed woman called “Vala” throughout the early portions of the journal as Vlaurunga, Primal Dragon of Fire. Whether every passage is authentic remains debated, but the journal remains the principal source for the legend of the first Metal Adventurer.*

### **17th Day of the Harvest Moon**

Met a woman on the road today. Perhaps “met” is too gentle a word. She fell out of a tree.

I heard shouting above me, then cracking branches, then suddenly this red-haired woman came crashing through an oak and landed directly in the middle of the road.

She stood up, brushed the leaves from her coat, and said, “That went better than expected.”

I asked what exactly she had expected.

“More branches.”

Her name is Vala. She says she is traveling east. I am traveling east. She has no horse, little food, no visible coin, and apparently no concern whatsoever about any of these facts. Against my better judgment, I have permitted her to accompany me. I suspect I shall regret this.

### **22nd Day of the Harvest Moon**

Vala has an unusual definition of caution. Three brigands stopped us today. Their leader demanded my purse. Before I could even reach for my sword, Vala punched him. Not stabbed. Not threatened. Punched. His companions drew their weapons. She seemed delighted. I have fought beside soldiers less enthusiastic about battle.

Afterward, while I was bandaging a cut on my arm, I asked why she had attacked before determining whether they might be reasoned with.

“They had swords.”

“So do we.”

“Yes.”

She stared at me as though this explained everything. I am beginning to understand why she travels alone.

### **4th Day of First Frost**

There is something strange about Vala. Tonight we camped beneath the old hills. The temperature dropped sharply after sunset, and I woke sometime after midnight because the fire had gone out, or at least I thought it had. Vala was asleep beside it. The logs were black. No flame remained. Yet the stones around her were warm. Not merely warm from the fire. Warm like summer sunlight. I placed my hand near hers and could feel heat coming from her skin. When I asked about it this morning, she laughed.

“I’m warm-blooded.”

“That is not what warm-blooded means.”

“It could be.” She refused to discuss it further.

### **19th Day of Deep Frost**

Saved Vala’s life today. I intend to remember this entry whenever she becomes intolerable. We were exploring ruins beneath the western hills when part of the ceiling collapsed. She pushed me out of the way and ended up beneath a stone beam. For once, she did not make a joke. I spent perhaps ten minutes levering the stone high enough for her to crawl free. Afterward she sat silently beside me. Then she said, very softly: “You know, most people would have run.” I told her most people did not owe her twelve silver pieces. She laughed. But there was something strange in her expression. Gratitude, certainly. But perhaps surprise. As if no one had ever needed to save her before.

### **3rd Day of New Flame**

I have traveled with Vala for almost two years. I no longer remember precisely when companionship became friendship. Perhaps it was after the goblin caves. Perhaps after the winter in Hareth. Perhaps after she carried me three miles with a spear wound through my thigh while complaining the entire journey that I was “surprisingly heavy for someone with such little sense.” We have fought monsters, bandits, sorcerers, one extremely territorial giant goat, and a minor lord who attempted to imprison us after Vala insulted his hat. I have seen her frightened exactly twice. Once when I nearly died. Once when a child fell from a bridge. Nothing else seems to trouble her. Fire certainly does not. I have stopped asking about that.

### **Undated Entry**

Today I learned the truth. I do not know how to write this. Vala is not Vala. Or perhaps she is. She insists that Vala is as much her name as any other. But she is Vlaurunga. <u>Vlaurunga.</u> The Dragon of Fire. The ancient one. The Mother of Flame. The being whose likeness is carved upon temple walls older than my grandfather’s grandfather’s language. We had reached the summit of Mount Cerath after completing what I believed would be our final adventure together. She told me she had something to show me. Then she changed. There is no other word for it. One moment stood my friend, the reckless woman who cheats at dice, burns every stew she attempts to cook, and once spent an entire evening trying to convince me that salamanders make excellent pets. The next, Fire. Wings spread across the sky. Scales like molten iron. Eyes brighter than a forge. I fell to my knees. She immediately became Vala again and told me to stop. I did not. She kicked me. “Get up.”

“You are Vlaurunga.”

“Yes.”

“The Vlaurunga.”

“I assume there is only one.”

I confess that I shouted several things after this which I shall not reproduce. She laughed through most of them. Then she embraced me, and for perhaps the first time since I have known her, Vlaurunga had nothing clever to say.

### **The Same Day, Later**

Before we parted, she gave me something. Not an object. A power. She placed her hand against my chest and told me that fire had always understood metal. Ore sleeps within stone. Flame wakes it. The hammer gives it purpose. Then she said: “You've spent years trying to protect me with that little sword of yours. Let's see what you can do when the sword listens.”

There was heat. For one terrible instant I thought my blood was boiling. Then everything stopped. I could feel my sword. Not the grip against my hand. The sword itself. Every piece of iron within it. I could feel the buckles on my pack. The nails in my boots. The coins in my purse. It was like suddenly discovering that the world had been whispering my entire life and I had only now learned how to hear it. Vlaurunga smiled. “Metal knows fire. Now it knows you.”

Then she left. No grand departure. No thunder. No pillar of flame. She simply waved, turned down the mountain path, and disappeared around the bend. I stood there for almost an hour. Then I attempted to move my sword without touching it. Nothing happened.

### **One Month After Vlaurunga's Gift**

Progress. Technically. Today I bent a spoon. Not very much. But I bent it. I stared at the thing for half an hour afterward. Then I tried again and gave myself a headache so severe that I vomited. If this is divine power, the divine may keep it.

### **Two Months After the Gift**

Bent another spoon. Unfortunately I was holding it at the time. Therefore I am uncertain whether magic was involved.

### **Three Months After the Gift**

I hate spoons.

### **Four Months After the Gift**

Something happened today. I placed a nail upon the table. I concentrated. The nail moved. Perhaps the width of my smallest fingernail. But it moved. I did not touch it. I laughed so loudly that the innkeeper came upstairs to see whether I had gone mad. When I explained, he asked me to demonstrate. Naturally, the nail refused to move again. I suspect metal has a sense of humor. Perhaps Vlaurunga put that in there.

### **Seventh Month**

I can reliably lift a copper coin. Iron is harder. Steel is much harder. I had assumed larger pieces would simply require greater effort, but I am beginning to think that shape matters. Purity matters. Heat matters. Hot metal is easier. Much easier. I placed a horseshoe in the forge today and could feel it almost as though it were an extension of my hand. I pulled it from the coals without tongs. The smith screamed. I screamed as well, although mostly because he screamed. The horseshoe floated between us. Neither of us spoke for some time. I believe I have finally begun to understand what Vlaurunga meant. Fire wakes metal.

### **Three Years After the Gift**

Bandits attacked the caravan outside Merrad. Twelve men. Perhaps thirteen. I drew my sword, as habit demanded. Then I realized they had drawn theirs. Metal. Every one. For the first time, I did not think of my gift. I simply acted. I pulled. Every sword left every hand at once. They rose perhaps twenty feet into the air and hung there. The bandits stared upward. So did I. One man ran. The others quickly followed. I stood beneath a dozen floating blades and began laughing. It took me another hour to get them down. One remains lodged in a tree. I have decided to leave it there.

### **Ninth Year**

People have begun calling me the Metal Adventurer. I dislike the title. Vlaurunga would love it. I can hear her already. “Could have been the Spoon Adventurer.”

Perhaps I should be grateful the name came later. My control has grown considerably. Armor is easiest when someone is wearing it. A breastplate gives me something coherent to grasp. Weapons can be turned. Locks opened. Chains broken. I can draw ore from loose earth now, though doing so exhausts me terribly. The strangest discovery came at the forge. I no longer need hammer and tongs for the simplest work. Metal bends beneath my hands without my hands touching it. A smith once told me that metal must be beaten into obedience. He was wrong. Metal does not obey. It listens. And if one learns to listen in return, one may persuade it. Perhaps that was the lesson all along.

### **Twenty-Seventh Year After the Gift**

I retired today. Properly this time. No more ruins. No more monsters. No more accepting contracts because someone says, “It should be easy.” Those words have nearly killed me seventeen times. There was a small ceremony in town. Far too many speeches. They called me Aderic Vale, the Metal Adventurer. Apparently the name has stuck. Children asked me to perform tricks. I made twelve spoons dance across the banquet table. No one understood why I found this so amusing. My sword hangs above the hearth now. I have not touched it in years. Sometimes, when the house is quiet, I make it drift down from the wall and circle the room. Not because I need practice. Just because I remember the first time I tried. And failed. And failed. And failed again.

### **Final Surviving Entry**

I am old. Older than I imagined I would become when Vala and I wandered the roads arguing over maps and sleeping beneath ruined towers. People ask whether I truly traveled with Vlaurunga. I tell them yes. They ask what she was like. They expect wisdom. Majesty. Fire. I tell them that she cheated at cards. That she hated waking early. That she once challenged a minotaur to a drinking contest and lost. That she believed every locked door was personally insulting her. That she laughed more loudly than anyone I ever knew. They are usually disappointed. They want the dragon. I knew the woman. Perhaps that was her final gift to me. Not the metal. Not the power. She let me know her. Occasionally, I still wonder where she went after that mountain. Somewhere out there, I suspect, an innkeeper is shouting at a red-haired traveler who has started a fight she absolutely did not need to start. And if she ever returns, I hope she visits. There is something I have wanted to show her for many years. I can finally bend the spoon.
