# **DRAGONS WALK AMONG US???**

### ***An Opinion Column Submitted to the Editors of* The Heraklion Lantern**

### ***By J. W. Pindlewick, Independent Dracological Investigator, Former Lecturer, Current Truth-Teller***

Citizens of Hurly—**WAKE UP.**

I do not write these words lightly. I write them with ink purchased from a man who *refused to tell me where he got it,* which is suspicious already. I write them from a room whose windows I have covered in copper mesh, whose floor I have salted, and whose chimney I have checked **three times** for winged serpentine eavesdroppers.

Because the truth is now too obvious to ignore:

## **THE SIX PRIMORDIAL DRAGONS ARE NOT GONE.**

## **THEY ARE HERE.**

## **THEY WALK AMONG US IN HUMANOID FORM.**

You were taught—*conveniently*—that the Divine Founders shaped the world and then withdrew from it. Ouro’ras made the peoples of light. Golestandt made the creatures of darkness. Vlaurunga gave rise to beings of flame and metal. Yvander formed the children of sea and cold. Quintara Lotus birthed the wondrous and arcane. Rokesh raised beasts, gems, and the proud Siliciae from earth itself. This is the common teaching. This is the nursery version. This is what they **want you comfortable believing.**

But I ask you this:

**Why would a dragon create a world, fill it with beloved children, and then simply leave?**

Would a sculptor abandon his masterpiece? Would a shepherd ignore his flock? Would a dragon—ancient, cunning, and terrible beyond mortal understanding—*not wish to see how their creation unfolds firsthand?*

No. No, I say! They are among us. They have taken shapes familiar to their own creations, slipping through our marketplaces, monasteries, courts, barracks, academies, and perhaps even **editorial offices**—yes, Marsten, I mean you.

And mark this well: **each Primordial takes the form of one of their own kindred peoples.** Not randomly. Not as birds. Not as suspiciously intelligent goats, as my cousin maintains. **They come as their children.** Their *kin.* Their *living signatures.*

## **OURO’RAS — THE LIGHT DRAGON — WALKS AS A HUMAN… OR WORSE, AN ELF**

Let us begin with Ouro’ras, the so-called Dragon of Light, creator of humans and many of the peoples who fancy themselves civilized, luminous, rational, and insufferably confident at dinner parties. Humans. Elves. Dwarves. Gnomes. Halflings. All trace their origin to his radiance.

So what form would Ouro’ras take?

A king? Too visible. A priest? Too predictable. A scholar? **Precisely.**

I submit that Ouro’ras lives presently in the guise of a mild, silver-haired elf who always seems to know when the weather will clear, who speaks fondly of “ancient customs” he could not possibly remember, and who squints directly into sunrise without watering eyes. You know the sort. Every village has one. Every academy protects one. No one questions him because he “has always been there.”

**OF COURSE HE HAS. HE WAS THERE AT CREATION.**

Others claim Ouro’ras favors human form—perhaps a wandering healer, perhaps a blind old beggar who sees more than all of us. I have personally recorded nine cases of strangers appearing after disasters, offering oddly specific moral advice, then departing before anyone learns their surname.

Coincidence?\
**That is a word used by people who do not keep notebooks.**

## **ROKESH — THE EARTH DRAGON — IS CERTAINLY A KOBOLD**

Here we meet the easiest case. Rokesh, Father of beasts, gems, and the Siliciae, is especially honored among the Kobolds, who regard themselves as children of his stone and strength. Their very culture, magic, and sacred traditions turn upon their relationship to him.

And yet we are expected to believe Rokesh does not walk among them?

Absurd.

If Rokesh has assumed mortal guise, then **he is a Kobold.** I will stake my reputation, my breakfast, and one sealed jar of preserved swamp eels upon it.

But what kind of Kobold?

Not an emperor. Too obvious. Not a general. Too public. Not a priest of the Kedgelony—though do not mistake me, I have concerns there. No, Rokesh would hide as someone beneath notice: an old tunnel mason, a quiet quarryman, a stooped vendor of pierres d’amour who always seems to know which stone belongs with which child.

Watch for the Kobold whose feet leave no dust. Watch for the Kobold who places a hand upon a wall moments before an earthquake ceases. Watch for the grandmother who scolds a cracked mountain face and then—**this has been reported to me**—the rockfall simply stops.

The Empire may boast of Metabolds and their earth-shaping power, but I ask you: **what if one among them is not merely chosen by Rokesh, but Rokesh himself?**

## **YVANDER — THE ICE DRAGON — HIDES AMONG THE LANYARUI**

Yvander, mother of sea creatures, iceborn beings, and the Aquisi, would never select some dry-skinned land-dweller as disguise. No. She walks among those nearest to tide, chill, and depth. The Lanyarui are obvious candidates, and that very obviousness is why scholars dismiss it—which is *exactly* what Yvander would count on.

Has no one noticed how certain Lanyarui sailors predict storms three days before the gulls change course? Has no one questioned the occasional sea-priestess whose breath fogs in tropical heat? Has no one demanded to know why there are fisherfolk in the northern reefs who speak of a woman in pale robes walking across harbor water at dawn?

“Folklore,” say the educated.\
“Rumors,” say the dockmasters.\
“Please leave,” say the coast guards.

But I know what I have compiled.

Yvander is among the Lanyarui. She may be a pearl-diver. She may be a queen in exile. She may be the silent passenger on a ferry who stares into the water for six hours and then vanishes before port.

**Do not accept drinks offered by unusually calm strangers during blizzards.**

## **VLAURUNGA — THE FIRE DRAGON — LIVES AS ONE OF THE FLAMEBORN**

Vlaurunga, mother of machines, metallic beings, and those born of fire, has perhaps hidden most cleverly of all. For who would question a Fire Genasi whose hair burns without fuel? Who would interrogate a master artificer who claims to have “a knack for engines”? Who would suspect a forge-worker whose hammer falls in the exact rhythm of a volcano?

I WOULD. I HAVE. THEY HAVE GENERALLY DECLINED TO ANSWER.

The family of Vlaurunga includes the flame-touched and metal-wrought: Fire Genasi, Tieflings, Zorat, and the vast lineage of crafted or mechanical beings attributed to her fiery creativity.

Thus, I propose Vlaurunga currently resides in one of three likely forms:

1.  A Fire Genasi industrialist who “revolutionizes” metallurgy every decade under a different name.

2.  A seemingly ordinary golem who has remained in service for six centuries and has begun making unsolicited philosophical observations.

3.  A Tiefling blacksmith whose ovens are never lit, yet are always hot.

Laugh if you like. But remember: every time civilization leaps forward through some astonishing new engine or impossible alloy, someone always says, *“The idea came to me in a dream.”*

**WHO SENT THE DREAM?**

## **QUINTARA LOTUS — THE ARCANE DRAGON — IS ABSOLUTELY DISGUISED AS AN AASIMAR, A FAIRY, OR SOMEONE WHO TEACHES “ADVANCED THEORY”**

Quintara Lotus, Dragon of the Arcane, maker of magical beings and mysteries, is the most dangerous to locate because **she would enjoy being sought.** She is the sort of divine intelligence that would leave clues, then alter the clues, then turn the seeker’s hat into a frog.

Her children include Aarakocra, Aasimar, Air Genasi, Changelings, Fairies, Satyrs, and others whose very existence suggests that the universe once paused and said, “What if whimsy had legal standing?”

Lotus may be a fairy archivist. Lotus may be an Aasimar fortune-teller who refuses payment but demands riddles. Lotus may be a changeling who has infiltrated every magical college simultaneously, arguing with herself in competing academic journals.

I have encountered one woman—name withheld because she gave me seven different ones—who answered every question I asked with another question, produced a lotus from a locked drawer, and then told me, **“You are closer than you think, but farther than you deserve.”**

If that was not Quintara Lotus, then it was someone who should be examined.

## **GOLESTANDT — THE DARK DRAGON — HIDES WHERE WE LEAST WANT TO LOOK**

Now we come to Golestandt. Darkness. Night. The shaper of Erosians, Twili, Husks, and all manner of shadowed things.

I know what you are thinking: “Surely Golestandt would not walk freely in Hurly, especially after all that has transpired.”

I answer: **that is exactly why he would.**

What better disguise for the Dragon of Darkness than someone who appears already mistrusted? A Twili exile. A quiet Husk who speaks more clearly than expected. A night watchman no one notices because he is supposed to be awake when all others sleep.

Golestandt would not announce himself with claws and thunder. He would sit in the corner of an inn. He would listen. He would remember what was said when men thought only shadows heard them.

Perhaps he appears as a Twili philosopher arguing that darkness is not evil but misunderstood. Perhaps he appears as a pale child who knows the names of stars invisible to the naked eye. Perhaps he appears in dreams only, then walks past you the next day in the market.

And consider this: if the Twili tell stories of shadow and longing, if the old myths remember Golestandt as one who listened inward rather than outward, then why should we imagine he ceased listening?

**HE IS LISTENING NOW.**

## **WHY DO THEY HIDE?**

Here we arrive at the question my many detractors sling at me from tavern doors:

“If the Dragons are among us, Pindlewick, why don’t they reveal themselves?”

Why, indeed?

Perhaps they test us. Perhaps they guide us subtly. Perhaps they judge whether their creations have become noble, monstrous, or merely bureaucratic. Perhaps they are divided among themselves, each influencing events through mortal guise, playing some vast draconic game of providence and catastrophe.

Or perhaps—and I urge you to sit before reading further—

**perhaps they have forgotten they are dragons.**

Yes. Yes! A divine self-limitation! A voluntary veiling! They live as one of their children so completely that they experience fear, hunger, longing, sorrow, love, paperwork, and the intolerable cost of city lodging. Each Primordial learns their creation from within.

Rokesh feels the ache of stone bones.\
Yvander tastes saltwater hunger.\
Vlaurunga burns with invention.\
Lotus dreams in riddles.\
Ouro’ras suffers the brightness of hope.\
Golestandt learns what it is to be feared.

And if they awaken—if all six realize themselves at once—

**WHAT THEN?**

A new creation?\
A judgment?\
A correction?\
A breakfast gathering of catastrophic theological consequence?

I do not know. I am not pretending to have *every* answer. I am merely the only columnist in Heraklion brave enough to ask the right questions.

## **WHAT SHOULD YOU DO?**

Remain vigilant.

Be polite to strangers.\
Do not insult old Kobolds near unstable masonry.\
Do not lie to mysterious elves who appear during sunrises.\
Do not mock an unusually elegant ferry passenger in winter.\
Do not kick an apparently inactive golem.\
Do not answer riddles posed by fairies unless you have cleared your afternoon.\
And never, under any circumstance, assume the quiet figure at the end of the bar is merely quiet.

Because the next person you meet may be a merchant, a priest, a refugee, a sailor, a mason, a smith, a scholar—

—or the ancient dragon who made your ancestors from the raw breath of creation.

## **DRAGONS WALK AMONG US.**

## **OPEN YOUR EYES.**
