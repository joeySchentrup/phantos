# First Story:

Long ago, just after the Great Hatching, The Divine Dragons, after creating the Twins, decided to create 15 lesser dragons that were combinations of their respective elements to rule over the combination magics. The first fourteen dragons were created, with the daughter of Ouro'ras and Golestandt, Jinshi: the Greater Dragon of Life, was appointed as the leader of these Greater Dragons. However, the last dragon took longer to be created, for Quintara Lotus truly loved Rokesh and wished not to create just any dragon to be their child, but rather wished to create a true child between them. So, to do this, Lotus created a gift to give to Rokesh, a creation using her power and the fruits of Rokesh’s power, and gave it as a gift to Rokesh’s beloved children, the Kobolds. These were the power stones, or in Kobold, the *pierres d’amour.*[^1]

When Lotus presented these to Rokesh, he loved them and, so also loved Lotus. The tow of them joined together and fell in love, being the only of the Divine Dragons to do so. Upon doing so, the fruit of their love was the last of the Greater Dragons, Agape: the Greater Dragon of Love. The two of them Loved their daughter, and their love shone through to the stones, which were empowered by their love.

Now these stones act as signs of love when given to other Kobolds and have an animalistic soul. These stones are treated like beloved pets by the Kobolds, and they frown on those who would use them for malevolent uses, for they should be treated with great respect.

# Second Story:[^2]

Long ago, in the age of dawn known as the Era of Awakening, when the world still trembled from the echoes of creation, the Divine Dragons emerged from the primordial forces to shape the realms. After the Great Hatching, the moment where the first conscious sparks lit the void, the Divine Dragons breathed life into existence, each embodying a primal element—Light, Dark, Fire, Ice, Arcane, and Earth.

Among their first creations were the Twins who acted as the keepers of the world's equilibrium. But even this was not enough, for the forces of the world were manifold, not separate, but interwoven. Thus, the Divine Dragons resolved to create fifteen Greater Dragons, each to embody and govern the sacred unions of elemental magic—fusion forces that danced between the lines of their original powers.

One by one, fourteen dragons were shaped from shared breath and magic, each the child of two Divine Dragons. Each dragon held domain over a specific combination magic: Lightning, Metal, Time, Plants, and so on. The leader among them was the wise and compassionate Jinshi, daughter of Ouro’ras, the Divine Dragon of Light, and Golestandt, the Divine Dragon of Darkness. Jinshi, bearing the radiant aspect of Life, was revered by all her siblings, for she did not only embody harmony—she nurtured it.

But the fifteenth seat remained empty.

This final dragon was to be born of Quintara Lotus, the Divine Dragon of the Arcane, and Rokesh, the Divine Dragon of Earth. Lotus, unlike her kin, did not wish to forge another being of elemental might alone. She had watched the others create their children with duty, power, and magic—but she longed for something more. Something real.

Lotus had long admired Rokesh from afar. His patience, his wisdom, his steady and enduring nature stirred something in her vast and secretive heart. And so, she resolved that their child would not be formed, but born—not merely a fusion of power, but the fruit of true love.

To express her affection, she created a gift—an offering not of grandeur, but of intimacy. Using her own Arcane-magic to shape the essence of love, and drawing upon the patient pulse of Rokesh's earthen strength, she crafted delicate, glowing stones—each one alive with a gentle warmth. These were the pierres d’amour—or “stones of love”, as the Kobolds, Rokesh’s cherished children, would come to call them.

She gave them not to Rokesh directly, but to his beloved Kobolds, knowing that he would see her heart in the act. The Kobolds were delighted with the stones, for each one shimmered with an inner soul, animalistic and affectionate, like tiny creatures of living gemstone. The Kobolds adored them, cherished them, and protected them as kin. And Rokesh saw. He understood. He felt. And for the first time, a Divine Dragon loved in return.

He joined with Lotus in harmony deeper than elemental bond. From their union—not from spell or summoning, but from true connection—was born the final Greater Dragon: Agape, the Dragon of Love.

Agape was unlike her siblings. Where the others ruled elements and forces, she ruled the unseen thread that tied all beings together. She could touch the hearts of mortals and gods alike. Flowers bloomed in her presence, even on stone. Beasts grew tame in her shadow. She could silence wars with a gaze, and inspire songs with a whisper.

Her presence did more than balance the others—it completed them.

And the pierres d’amour, now blessed by her very essence, were transformed. They became living relics, vessels of empathy and connection. Among the Kobolds, they grew into sacred companions—neither tools nor weapons, but beloved pets and spiritual kin. Each stone had its own will, its own subtle voice, and would only bond with those whose hearts were pure and intentions sincere.

The Kobolds, a humble and heartful people, came to believe that to give such a stone was the highest form of affection—be it friendship, familial love, or romantic devotion. To misuse a pierre d’amour, to treat it as a commodity or weapon, was to defile the love of the dragons themselves, a sacrilege punishable not by law, but by shame and exile.

Even now, in the modern age, when myths fade into story and dragons hide behind the veil of time, the Kobolds remember. They tell the tale of Agape, the Dragon of Love, and the stones that carry her soul.

And on quiet nights, when the wind hums through the caverns, they say you can hear her voice, soft and warm, calling out from the heart of the world.

[^1]: The Kobold name for this is most properly translated as ‘love stones,’ but because of the secrecy that the Kobolds had about them the other races only knew of their power, and so they became known in common as ‘power stones.’

[^2]: The reason for the two stories is believed to be because the first story arose first as a simple retelling and then the second was created as a way to tell the story with more passion.
