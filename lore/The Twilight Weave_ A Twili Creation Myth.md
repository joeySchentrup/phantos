## **🌑 *The Twilight Weave: A Twili Creation Myth***

In the beginning, before shadow and starlight danced across the land, there was silence—and from that silence came Golestandt, the Primal Dragon of Darkness. While his kin sculpted life from light and flame, sea and stone, Golestandt looked not outward, but inward. He listened to the hush between heartbeats, the stillness under mountains, the mystery of night.

From these, he wove his creations—creatures of dusk and silence, not meant for the burning sun, but for the cool serenity of shadow. Among them were the first of the Twili: beings not of one shape or place, but of transitions—between light and dark, between seen and unseen, between the breath and the whisper.

These early Twili were born from pools of moonless water and cloaked mist. Golestandt did not teach them to speak as others did. Instead, he gave them the language of silence, gesture, and dreaming. They walked softly, and wherever their feet touched, twilight followed.

When the Six Primal Dragons gathered and poured their final magics into the world, Golestandt offered something special. While others made dragons of fusion and flame, he instead whispered into the void and called forth two children—one of blazing joy, the other of quiet power as the culmination of all the powers of the 6 dragons.

The one born of day was Quint, the Radiant Fool. The one born of night was Erosia, the Hidden Mirror.

Quint laughed and danced through the sky, and mortals sang his name with trumpets and fire. Erosia walked unseen, guiding dreams, wrapping the world in starlight, and cradling secrets. The Twili beheld her not as a god, but as a sister. For she, too, belonged to the space in-between.

Erosia loved the Twili as Golestandt had. She saw in them a reflection of herself—misunderstood, unpraised, but powerful. She taught them to thrive in the darkness, not as exiles, but as chosen. She gifted them nocturnal vision, the ability to move through shadow, and most sacred of all, the Moonveil Rite—the power to become half-light, half-thought, disappearing where others would be seen.

But the people of the day feared the night. And they feared the Twili.

They called them spies, ghosts, monsters. They shut their gates at dusk and whispered lies about what slithered in the dark. And always, they worshiped Quint, whose light blinded them to what they could not understand.

Thus did Erosia grow sorrowful… and then angry. She whispered to the Twili a new dream—not of coexistence, but of reclamation. “Why must we be hidden?” she asked. “Why must night always yield to day?”

The Twili, loyal and proud, followed her into the first rebellion.

And so began the Erosian War, the first night that did not end. For a time, the world was cloaked in endless twilight. The Twili walked freely in cities that had long shunned them, their shadows swallowing the banners of day. But Quint, fool though he was, was not powerless. With the help of the Draconic Champions, he struck Erosia down, and she was banished to the Twilight Realm, taking her children with her.

Yet the Twili did not curse her for their exile. They did not forget her. In the endless dusk of that realm-between-worlds, they built their cities anew—woven from shadowstuff, starlight, and silence. They remembered Golestandt, their maker, and Erosia, their mother and queen. And they vowed to keep the night sacred, to hone their gifts, and to wait.

For prophecy says:

> *When one of the light who follows the dark lights the bridge for darkness to return, Erosia shall rise again. And with her, the Twili shall walk freely beneath both moon and sun, as was always their birthright.*

Until then, they train in shadow. They watch. They wait.

And when they move, even the stars hold their breath.
